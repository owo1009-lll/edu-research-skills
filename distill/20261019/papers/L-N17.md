# L-N17: Show how an educational concept changes an ordinary teaching decision

Status: new English development source, collaborative autoethnography. The two authors analyze their own participation and teaching. This is a qualitative empirical account, not a controlled professional-development evaluation, a survey, or independent observation of all learning-community members.

## Question, setting and the role of the authors

The question concerns how autoethnographic reflection mediates two international faculty members' learning through a year-long faculty learning community (FLC). The group met ten times for two hours, using readings, guest speakers, discussions, presentations, peer feedback and role-play. The two authors were visiting assistant professors, but entered with different concerns. Henny wanted to reconsider teaching that had become familiar under a heavy teaching and grading workload. Galina wanted to reconsider how international status and accented English related to her legitimacy as a language-teaching role model.

Those concerns give the two narratives different argumentative work. Henny develops a relational account of teaching; Galina reinterprets a stigmatized characteristic as a professional resource. The common setting does not require identical changes or a single standardized outcome. The comparison is useful precisely because it preserves the different problems each author was trying to understand.

The methods describe independent narrative writing, regular mutual discussion, feedback and notes, followed by shared interpretation. Personal memories, conversations and reflection form the main evidence. Collaboration is an interpretive process, not a claim that two independent observers confirmed every classroom event. The publication gives relatively little detail about a particular disagreement between the authors or how feedback changed a final interpretation. It should not be rewritten as a fully displayed collaborative audit trail.

## Concept-to-action sequence

Henny's story moves from her earlier view of serious, content-focused teaching to reconsidering students' contributions. Her account locates that view in specific arrangements: co-taught courses, shared pacing, common assessments and the consequences of student evaluations. These details do explanatory work; they prevent the change from appearing to be a purely personal discovery detached from teaching conditions.

The conceptual turn is teaching as a relationship among teachers, students and communication. Two episodes make it intelligible:

- Students assess earlier essays with the course rubric. Differences between student and teacher judgments become information about what students consider good writing, which Henny uses when explaining expectations. The learning here includes the teacher's learning from students; it is not simply a claim that peer grading improves attainment.
- Li, a pseudonym, points out that supplied sample paragraphs do not match an assignment requirement. Henny supplies examples addressing the requirement and explicitly credits the student's observation in a course announcement. She explains why she chose to name that contribution: acknowledging her own fallibility and inviting students to identify how teaching materials could serve them better.

The second episode is especially economical. A short artifact shows a changed instructional decision; the following paragraph explains what that decision means for the teacher–student relationship. The interpretation adds something beyond the announcement's literal content. It identifies whose contribution counts in shaping instruction.

Galina's story has a different turning point. An evaluation comment treats accented speech as incompatible with teaching English. She contrasts that comment with her recollection of apparently positive classroom interaction, then connects her insecurity to the literature as she understands it. FLC discussions, a simulation and reflection help her reconsider the issue. She introduces classroom conversations about English varieties, code-meshing and writing identity, and shares her own language-learning difficulties. A student's request for advice then illustrates recognition of her experience as a resource.

The student email is evidence of a request for guidance. Its value lies in the relation it makes visible, not in proving a measured change in language proficiency or the disappearance of accent prejudice. The original critical evaluation remains part of the story; deleting it would remove the problem that gives the reinterpretation its force.

## Sentence and paragraph analysis

The first paragraph on printed p. 15, immediately after the course announcement, contains seven sentences. Each advances the interpretation:

| Sentence | Added work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Identifies the student's email and the mismatch it raised. The artifact now has a triggering event. |
| 2 | Reveals the teacher's initial intention not to name the student. This makes the subsequent choice consequential. |
| 3 | Explains the reconsideration: the teacher wants to make the source of her new understanding visible. |
| 4 | Signals two purposes for that decision. This is a local organizational device, not a required count for other paragraphs. |
| 5 | Interprets public acknowledgement as making teacher fallibility discussable. |
| 6 | Connects that openness to students identifying improvements in materials. The relationship has an instructional consequence. |
| 7 | Returns to the relational concept and extends the intended benefit toward learning. The enacted contribution is clearer than the unmeasured downstream learning claim. |

At sentence level, the first-person subject is doing epistemic work. Henny distinguishes what she initially intended, what she reconsidered and why she acted. Turning the passage into impersonal claims that a learning community transformed teaching would remove both the decision and the author's responsibility for interpreting it. Strong verbs such as *identified*, *added* and *credited* can report the account directly; uncertainty belongs with a further claim about its effects.

The next paragraph places her position beside earlier FLC writing. It first recalls the literature's emphasis on student learning, then states how her account reconceptualizes teaching, and finally distinguishes her relational emphasis from an individualized reading of student-centered practice. This is a conceptual comparison with a clear author position. The earlier studies were not independently read here, so their precise argument cannot be certified. The useful move is to specify what concept is being extended rather than merely state agreement with previous research.

Galina's printed p. 16 paragraph about entering the FLC begins with her unsettled question, introduces peers' accounts, broadens accent to social and disciplinary judgments, and explains how discussion provided a place to speak even without a simple answer. The paragraph's resolution is a different relation to the problem, not the discovery that all prejudice has ended. That is a substantive qualitative claim and does not need a statistical test to be worth stating.

## Whole-article organization and voice

The article begins with the method and a shared experience, then gives professional-learning and FLC context. Methods identify the authors' positions and reflective process. Two separately titled stories carry most of the findings and interpretation. The conclusion reconnects their different learning to future community design.

This structure fits a question about personal interpretation in a shared setting. It would not automatically fit an observational ethnography with many interacting participants. The first-person voices, reproduced artifacts and separate story trajectories should survive editing because they carry the evidence and conceptual difference.

The early literature section accumulates many favorable outcomes of FLCs. A revision could compress that catalogue around the distinction most relevant here: facilitator accounts of organized activities versus participants' accounts of how those activities acquire meaning in their teaching. That would make the eventual narratives easier to anticipate without imposing a fixed number of gaps or research questions.

The two conclusions should also remain distinguishable. Henny's relationship argument is not simply reduced to the claim that she became more student-centered; Galina's argument is not reduced to a general increase in confidence. The conceptual additions are students' contribution to pedagogical decisions and the professional value of multilingual experience. Both retain the authors' position toward teaching and participation.

## Variation, limits and source claims retained

The conclusion acknowledges that not every activity produced meaningful reflection and that personal context mattered. This qualification helps explain the recommendation to provide varied reflective activities. It is more informative than a generic reminder that two people cannot represent everyone.

The account supports changes the authors describe in their interpretation and teaching. It does not isolate the effect of the FLC from relocation, employment conditions, other experiences or the later autoethnographic work. An editor should retain the temporal and interpretive narrative without turning it into a controlled causal evaluation.

The text's claim to be the first reported U.S. FLC of this kind is not independently verified. Its description of Indonesian teaching and evaluation reflects Henny's experience; it is not a nationwide comparative study. Similarly, an international student's negative evaluation does not establish what international students generally believe. The negative comment and the later supportive messages can both be retained without assigning either view to a whole population.

The conclusion's claim that only autoethnography allowed the authors to connect FLC experience with teaching should remain a situated account of their reflective process, not proof that other methods could not do so. No requirement for saturation, interrater agreement or statistical generalization is added to this autoethnographic design.

## Original English same-fact edits

These are newly composed third-person illustrations of Henny's published account, not fabricated first-person quotations. They use the same sequence: Li identifies the mismatch, Henny adds examples, she initially considers omitting attribution, and then credits the contribution to make student participation visible. No new student outcome is added.

**Before: list events and repeat a broad label.**

> Henny regarded students as collaborators in teaching. Li, a pseudonym, asked why the supplied sample paragraphs differed from the assignment requirements. Henny added two examples that addressed the requirement. She initially did not plan to name Li in the course announcement but then decided to acknowledge the contribution. She wanted students to recognize that teachers can make mistakes and that students can help improve materials. This episode concerned collaboration and the relationship between the teacher and students.

**After: make the decision explain the concept.**

> Henny made student collaboration visible in how she corrected her teaching materials. After Li pointed out that the sample paragraphs did not match the assignment requirements, she added two examples addressing the requirement. She first considered leaving Li unnamed, then credited the contribution in her announcement. That choice expressed her relational view of teaching: students could identify a problem, influence the materials used in class and see their contribution acknowledged. Teacher fallibility became an opening for participation.

**Compressed.**

> After Li identified a mismatch between sample paragraphs and assignment requirements, Henny added two relevant examples and chose to credit the contribution publicly. Her account makes the relational view of teaching concrete: a student's criticism changed instructional materials, and the teacher made that influence visible.

The edit gives the author's choice an explanatory role and removes the redundant declaration that the episode concerns collaboration. Compression keeps the triggering criticism, material change and attribution. It preserves the relational concept without inventing an attainment gain. In an actual revision of the authors' manuscript, their first-person voice would normally be retained rather than converted to this third-person teaching illustration.

## Relation to the qualitative development comparison

[L-N15](L-N15.md) connects teachers' prior experience to a newly encountered role. [L-N13](L-N13.md) traces what documents do in institutional practice. L-N17 adds a different form: a reflexive decision and a reproduced teaching artifact make a concept visible within the author's own account. The action is not to insert an anecdote into every paragraph. It is to select an episode whose details explain the judgment, then show what the interpretation adds to the episode.

This supports the existing route's attention to concrete action, participant meaning and author interpretation. The earlier L-C01 test did not show an advantage for another appended illustration, so this reading alone does not justify adding more runtime text. It remains a source-specific development demonstration and a possible future transfer context, not a final holdout or evidence of improved skill performance.
