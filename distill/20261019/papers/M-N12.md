## Why this source is useful

The source illustrates a move worth testing in writing: when a summary conceals an informative difference, change the comparison before polishing the conclusion. Its relevance is not a general endorsement of elaborate joint displays. An attractive arrangement can still compare the wrong objects or conceal an exception.

The study connects questionnaire-based case selection with classroom evidence. Richard's observed teaching differs from his initial classification; the authors interpret this through a distinction between espoused and ingrained beliefs (pp. 9–11). The displayed ratings and notes concern the same lessons.

An editor should retain the authors' conceptual distinction while making its evidential role clear. Calling an interpretation an interpretation need not weaken the conceptual contribution. It identifies the reasoning that makes the contribution available for scrutiny. Conversely, agreement between two representations of one event should not be presented as an independent replication. This is a distinction between evidential relationships, not a hierarchy in which numerical information outranks observational interpretation.

## A paragraph that makes an analytic decision intelligible

The five-sentence paragraph beginning the reflections on the third iteration, pp. 6–7, is worth reading as a sequence of argumentative functions:

| Sentence | Increment |
|---|---|
| 1 | Moves from rearrangement to analytic insight. |
| 2 | Makes a pattern the reason to revisit measurement. |
| 3 | Identifies the dimensions selected for closer attention. |
| 4 | Explains what that selection helps distinguish. |
| 5 | Reinforces the preceding decision through a counterfactual. |

The middle of the paragraph does the most useful work. It lets a reader ask whether the selected distinction answers the research question. An edit should protect that link, even if the opening or ending is shortened. Cutting only the final qualification or replacing the whole passage with “integration deepened understanding” would lose the inspectable decision.

At sentence level, an analyst acting on a specific object is easier to follow than an accumulation of methodological nouns. A useful revision question is: what did the researchers reconsider, and what became intelligible as a result? This is not a requirement that every sentence begin with “we,” nor that every paragraph recount a procedure. It is a way to locate the reasoning that the prose needs to carry.

The final counterfactual deserves a different editorial judgment from the intervening explanation. An author's confidence that a discovery would otherwise have been missed does not establish that no alternative analysis could reveal it. Retain the demonstrated usefulness of the procedure without turning it into a necessity claim. This preserves a positive methodological position while repairing a specific extension of it.

## Whole-article judgment and author voice

The repeated movement between a display, its interpretation and the next revision suits this article's instructional purpose. In a substantive findings article, reproducing the same amount of development history could delay the educational argument. The transferable skill is therefore selective explanation of consequential decisions, not copying the article's repeated subsection pattern.

For writing practice, distinguish three editorial decisions. First, retain enough development history for the reader to understand why the final comparison was chosen. Second, preserve the example that complicates the group description. Third, explain the resulting conceptual distinction where it changes the interpretation. These decisions can be made without requiring every intermediate image to enter the final manuscript.

The authors' first-person account is appropriate to an explanation of analytic work. Replacing it wholesale with passive constructions would obscure responsibility for choices. Likewise, changing every confident methodological claim into a possibility would not address the real issue. The relevant distinction is between an evidenced advantage in this application and a universal requirement for future studies.

There is also an opportunity for substantive compression: a sentence that explains the relationship between evidence and interpretation carries more value than several sentences announcing that integration is valuable. Compression should remove repeated announcements first. It should preserve the inferential step and the counterexample that makes that step necessary.

## Original same-fact English editing illustration

These are newly composed illustrations, not quotations, author prose or controlled model outputs. They demonstrate how to make a single interpretive relationship explicit without adding an outcome, motive or new observation.

**Before.**

> Richard belonged to the teacher-centered group. His observed practice was more learner-centered. The authors distinguished espoused from ingrained beliefs. This distinction was important for interpretation.

**After.**

> Richard's learner-centered practice complicated his teacher-centered survey classification. The authors used the distinction between espoused and ingrained beliefs to interpret this discrepancy.

**Compressed.**

> Richard's learner-centered practice contrasted with his teacher-centered survey profile, informing the authors' distinction between espoused and ingrained beliefs.

The revised version places the discrepancy before its interpretation and names the authors as interpreters. Compression retains both directions and the attribution, while removing a generic statement of importance. The surrounding manuscript still needs to supply the classroom evidence: this sentence connects evidence with interpretation rather than replacing the findings. Brevity is useful here because the inference remains visible.

An actual edit should also retain surrounding cases that complicate the theoretical account. An isolated illustration is a teaching device, not permission to turn the manuscript into a single exemplary anecdote. The unresolved issue is how far the conceptual reading accounts for variation, rather than how often a favored integration label appears.

## What not to copy

Figure 2's orange/purple text gives lesson numbers inconsistent with its legend. Figure 6 prints a middle category of 1–15, whereas earlier figures use 11–15. These were checked in the original images.

Neither inconsistency warrants silently repairing the source or dismissing its analytic contribution. They do show why visual correspondence must be checked at the level of the represented object, not inferred from matching colors. The analysis also should not treat a theoretical label as direct access to an unobserved mental state. Alternative interpretations remain available for examination; the source's preferred framework can remain visible without being declared uniquely established.

Do not turn descending bar order, a fixed number of excerpts or a fixed display sequence into general writing requirements. Their value depends on the comparison and the reader's task. This reading supplies a situated positive example and counterexamples under the existing mixed-research route. It does not establish a new runtime improvement.
