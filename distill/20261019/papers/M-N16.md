## What is integrated, and what is actually reported?

Table 1 describes quantitative records for 10,130 youth and adult education (YAE) students, 41 policy documents, qualitative work with 40 students, 18 teachers and eight principals, and a final participatory phase. The table lists three analytic roundtables and one virtual conference, involving four students, eight teachers, four principals, two policymakers and two experts. Section 3.3 separately reports 310 seminar viewers; that audience count is not the number of analytic participants.

The first integration uses demographic and policy analyses to inform the selection of schools for ethnographic inquiry (p. 5, end of §3.1). Statistical risk descriptions are then considered alongside students' accounts of exclusion, teachers' practices and institutional conditions (§3.2). Participants and researchers subsequently examine findings together to formulate recommendations (§3.3). This goes beyond merely placing numbers and interviews in adjacent sections, although the article often summarizes the links rather than demonstrating them with detailed evidence.

Figure 1 arranges student, school and system sectors across three concentric levels. It connects reported risk/protective factors, experiences and practices, and proposed responses. For example, the school sector brings resource and staffing conditions into relation with teacher identity and community, care and relationships. The display is conceptual: its radii, sectors and arrows do not estimate effect sizes or causal paths. The paper's normative commitment is to change exclusionary institutions and support educational rights. That commitment should survive editing; claims that the resulting recommendations have already improved retention would require different evidence.

## Sentence work: make an identity visible through practice

The paragraph beginning with teachers' disciplinary identity on p. 6, left column, contains five sentences. It follows students' painful recollections of exclusion, so the teachers' response has a concrete problem to address.

| Sentence | Added work in context | What can transfer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduces YAE teachers' unfamiliar professional setting and adaptation. | Name whose identity is at issue and the situation in which it matters. |
| 2 | Connects teachers' critical view of schooling to the inequalities they encounter. | Explain the content and object of a stance instead of naming a theoretical orientation alone. |
| 3 | Moves from critique to flexible teaching and support. | Give the position practical consequences. |
| 4 | Relates that support to students' distinctive needs and sociocultural circumstances. | Keep the recipients and purpose of action visible. |
| 5 | Specifies acknowledging varied trajectories, tailoring teaching, mediating, adapting curricula and making time to talk. | Let concrete verbs carry the conceptual claim. |

The paragraph's subjects remain teachers and students as its verbs move from professional interpretation to action. The final sentence earns its space by showing what flexibility entails. A rewrite consisting only of an identity label would lose that explanatory work. Conversely, the source's description of an ideal learning space is stronger than the evidence shown in this methodological account; the concrete practices can be retained without adopting that evaluative absolute. No chronology of identity development should be invented from these summarized accounts.

## Paragraph work: conditions, experience, then a response

The last paragraph before Figure 1 on p. 7 has five sentences. It follows a paragraph on precarious funding and long-term support.

1. The opening extends the account from economic conditions to their subjective consequences for the community. Its backward reference makes this an addition to the preceding argument, not a fresh declaration that the problem is complex.
2. The next sentence identifies the consequence as teachers' emotional and moral burden. An abstract level of analysis becomes a named group and experience.
3. The third sentence retains participants' sense that the problem is common while introducing their judgment that it can be recognized and addressed. This shifts from diagnosis to agency without making the experience disappear.
4. The fourth sentence links the diagnosis and participants' interpretations to developing recommendations.
5. The final sentence specifies two strategies: a caring, safe educational space and collective agency. Naming the proposals gives the preceding movement toward action a concrete destination.

The important inference is that a response must engage with both institutional conditions and relationships, rather than treating dropout only as an attribute of individual students. The article locates that inference in a participatory process. It does not establish that each statistical factor was matched to an individual interview or that the proposed responses reduced dropout. The wording should keep those distinctions close to the relevant claim, without repeatedly interrupting the paragraph with generic disclaimers.

The source calls the subsequent strategies new, but a reader cannot assess their novelty or effectiveness from their labels alone. The next useful writing step is to identify their specific content and how the shared analysis informed it—not to intensify the novelty adjective.

## Whole-article progression and tradeoffs

The opening introduces a distinction between understanding a problem and participating in its transformation. The account of Chilean YAE then gives that position an educational object: students returning after exclusion from mainstream schooling. The design section connects this object to student, school and policy levels. Results move from contextual analysis to risk descriptions, lived experience and collaborative interpretation. The discussion returns to the original methodological claim and a rights-oriented position.

This is a methodological article's organization, not a universal structure for mixed-methods empirical papers. Its strongest continuity is the repeated connection between exclusion, participation and institutional response. Its weakest economy is repeated praise of integration in §§3.2.2, 3.3 and 4: several paragraphs restate breadth, depth or transformative potential without adding a distinct comparison or episode. Compressing those statements could create room for evidence of how a participant's interpretation changed a recommendation. That evidence is not supplied here, so an editor cannot create the episode.

The extensive opening contrast among quantitative, qualitative and mixed approaches also makes overbroad claims. In particular, p. 1 presents qualitative trustworthiness/validity as replaced by other criteria, whereas p. 8 explicitly includes qualitative trustworthiness in rigor. The within-paper inconsistency is a direct reason not to adopt the earlier formulation. Nor should quantitative work be equated with context-free measurement or qualitative work with an inability to examine institutions. Preserve the authors' chosen transformative stance while assessing the specific argument used to support it.

## Same-fact editing illustrations in English

Both pairs below are newly composed from the article's reported material, not author quotations or independently generated test outputs. They demonstrate editorial choices; they are not evidence that a new skill rule improves behavior.

### Replace generalized integration praise with the work it did

**Before — deliberately diffuse illustration**

> The study used quantitative and qualitative methods to obtain a broad and deep understanding of exclusion. Quantitative data provided extensive knowledge, while qualitative data provided intensive knowledge. Integrating these perspectives made the study more comprehensive. The building stage involved the educational community in interpreting the findings and developing recommendations. These recommendations addressed school dropout through a transformative perspective.

**After — same facts, a specific connection**

> Demographic and policy analyses informed the selection of schools for closer study. Accounts of exclusion and teachers' responses then added how institutional conditions were experienced and addressed in YAE. Students, teachers and other participants worked with the researchers to interpret these findings and develop recommendations centred on care and collective agency. Integration therefore informed both what the study examined and how the community framed its response to dropout.

The revision gives the analyses distinct jobs and makes the contribution of their connection explicit. It keeps the authors' participatory position and the status of recommendations. It adds neither an observed reduction in dropout nor a claim that every participant agreed.

### Compress an explanation while preserving the author's position

**Before — newly composed illustration**

> Teachers in youth and adult education encountered a setting that was often unfamiliar to them. Their critical view of the education system developed alongside attention to the inequalities faced by their students. They responded through flexible teaching practices. These practices recognized that students followed different educational trajectories and had different needs. Teachers adjusted curricula, mediated difficulties and made themselves available for conversation. Such work linked criticism of exclusion to practical efforts to support students returning to education.

**After — compressed illustration**

> In youth and adult education, teachers connected their criticism of exclusion with flexible support for students returning to school. Recognizing different educational trajectories, they adapted curricula, mediated difficulties and made time to talk. Their stance took shape in how they responded to students' needs.

The compression retains critique, varied trajectories and concrete actions. It removes the unfamiliar-setting detail for a passage about enacted support; that detail would remain necessary in a paragraph about professional adaptation. It preserves an interpretive claim about practice, not a measured intervention effect.

## Reporting limits and development decision

- The source alternates between three phases, four enumerated activities and four collective instances. These may describe different levels of organization; do not silently treat them as interchangeable counts.
- The literature-search totals on pp. 3–4 do not provide a fully reproducible search and deduplication account. They cannot establish the absence of relevant prior work by themselves.
- The article summarizes quantitative predictors without their model estimates and participant experiences without extended evidentiary excerpts. Its component publications need reading before independently comparing those findings or learning their detailed reporting.
- The joint display supports an account of connections; its presence alone does not validate rigor, novelty or successful transformation. The final paragraph proposes following the actions in later work, consistent with outcomes still needing study.
- The displayed seminar link and the cited originals were not followed. Bibliographic entries have been read as part of the article, not independently verified as support for their associated claims.

For group comparison, this extends M-N09's distinction between opportunities to speak and what contributions do: participants here also help formulate responses. It differs from M-N15's theme-to-questionnaire and joint-display integration. These are different functions, not competing levels on a quality ladder. The existing integration and author-voice guidance already permits both. Keep the useful local examples in development notes; do not add a generic requirement to use participatory research, a circular display, a metaphor, or a prescribed paragraph sequence.
