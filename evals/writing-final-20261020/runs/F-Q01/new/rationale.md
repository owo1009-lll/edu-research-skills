# New-writing rationale

Prepared before drafting; scope: the supplied introduction, design, and results only.

Educational question: Does requiring university students to retrieve answers from memory improve delayed learning beyond answering the same questions while consulting materials?

Evidence answer: Closed-book practice produced higher surprise-quiz scores after delays of one to seven weeks. The additional module-exam comparison favored closed-book practice on related, previously unasked questions, including after adjustment for unrelated exam performance.

Core contribution: A classroom comparison against an active, question-matched alternative makes the retrieval requirement educationally consequential beyond the contrast with passive restudy. This is evidence for a practice arrangement, not direct identification of a cognitive mechanism.

Scope: First-semester psychology students in two parallel seminars at one university; seven practice sessions on different material. The effect of delay and the effect of multiple sessions were not independently manipulated.

Support: Random allocation; same instructor, script and questions; active comparator; blind scoring; effects on primary and additional related outcomes; adjusted exam result.

Counterweight: Small sample, unequal surprise-quiz attendance (27/30 open-book; 19/29 closed-book), one seminar per condition; no measured retrieval process or later study behavior; no exam-to-quiz individual linkage; no confidence intervals in supplied results; common quiz and feedback preceded module exam.

Terminology: closed-book practice; open-book practice; surprise quiz (primary outcome); related module-exam questions (additional outcome); retrieval practice (proposed explanation).
