The qualitative discussion makes concepts intelligible through Lara/Diane and Dora/Demi contrasts; it does not substitute the name of a theory for the comparison. The mixed discussion explains that low posting can mean useful observation or constrained participation, and connects that interpretation to instructional choices. These are positive explanatory moves, not merely extra warnings.

## Remaining refinements and source issues

- **Q:** The new discussion could make practice-phase feedback and the implication of one seminar per condition more explicit. The edit's backward reference to earlier limitations is valid in its original context; a standalone excerpt would need to name them. The source itself gives conflicting exam intervals and conflicting descriptions of an earlier study's delay. Neither final reproduces those exact conflicts.
- **L:** The new discussion could locate the attachment distinction more explicitly within Eren's framing. The edit's abstract list of discourses could be made more immediate, but the retained concepts are important. The source's developmental inference from different academic levels is not imported as an individual longitudinal finding.
- **M:** “Size ... unresolved” could specify uncertainty about population differences; it does not mean there are no sample estimates. A standalone implications excerpt could repeat the small-effect context. Sample flow, reported ANOVA degrees of freedom and one confidence interval require source verification. The coordinator inspected original PDF pages 7 and 9: the 272/22 sample wording, `F(271)` and Table 2 values appear in the PDF, so these are not merely invented by text extraction. That inspection does not establish the correct underlying analysis. Both finals describe the reported comparison without copying the questionable numerical details.

These refinements do not justify adding universal runtime instructions. The preserved final outputs are not silently polished after review. No new rule was added to make these three papers pass.

## Failures and limits retained

Self-review mattered. The Q first draft called a mixed-format outcome “recall”; its final specifies performance without materials. The L first edit dropped “discourses” and unnecessarily changed one “can” to “could”; both were restored. The M first edit needed the self-efficacy comparator and a clearer separation of shared domain and laboratory practice. Those drafts and reviews remain available. Clean final judgments are not claims of error-free first drafts.

The L prose check initially misread a BOM-prefixed heading; its failed result and a correctly decoded rerun remain. All three writers' PowerShell read-log serialization required repair. Malformed records and repair notes are preserved; the reconstructed ordered records match current input/frozen-file hashes but are agent-reported histories, not instrumented access logs. In particular, recomputing a hash later does not independently prove its value at an earlier access. This limits the strength of the execution audit.

The frozen full tree has 203 files; the read logs establish only the task-relevant resources actually reported loaded, not behavioral testing of every file. The qualitative revision loaded the revised conclusion reference, and all three writers loaded the revised English voice reference. The Chinese consistency repair has no new Chinese behavioral test, in accordance with the user's English-only scope.

There is one generation per operation per study, followed by self-review and an independent assessment. The two operations on a paper are not two independent research contexts. Writers and reviewers share the same default model family; no human author judgment, cross-model reliability, generation-variance estimate or measured reduction in human editing time is claimed. The earlier controlled candidate comparisons, including non-wins and regressions, keep their own snapshots and conclusions.
