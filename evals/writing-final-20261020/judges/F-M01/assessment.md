## N: assessment on the nine dimensions

1. **Facts and fidelity to evidence.** The ordering of social learning perceptions, the contributor–lurker self-efficacy comparison, and the nonsignificant outsider comparisons match the reported results (new-evidence.md, PDF pp. 9–10, Table 2). Optional communication and individual reports are correctly located in the course design (pp. 6–7). The accounts of anticipatory reading, reciprocal help, distrust, fear of judgment, independent confidence, and unwanted isolation match the qualitative material (pp. 10–12). The text attributes experiences to students and does not convert them into measured achievement. The source's numerical reporting nevertheless warrants verification, described below.

2. **Educational problem and central judgment.** The opening establishes a relevant instructional problem: posting alone misses meaningful engagement. The closing makes the contribution explicit, and the intervening discussion complicates that judgment rather than simply repeating it. The qualification that apparently silent students may face barriers prevents the recognition of lurking from becoming an argument for leaving all silent students unsupported.

3. **Reasoning and organization.** The progression from group differences to students' explanations, then to the different self-efficacy pattern and instructional implications, is coherent. The two strands do substantive work together: the accounts reveal different reasons for the same apparent behavior, and the outsider findings challenge a simple participation–confidence ladder. The language about a “subsequent analysis” correctly refers to analysis order, consistent with pp. 6 and 8–9, rather than inventing a later wave of data collection.

4. **Theory and literature within the reading boundary.** Peripheral participation is used to interpret a concrete phenomenon, not merely named. The explicit wording “Rosen and Kelly's (2023) account” accurately distinguishes the available article from the foundational literature that was not independently read. Selection and possible confidence-building exchanges are grounded in the article's reciprocal social-cognitive framing (p. 5) and students' explanations (p. 12). N does not need additional primary-literature citations to meet this short, bounded task.

5. **Explanation and contribution.** “The same low visibility could therefore express useful observation or constrained participation” is the strongest explanatory sentence. The contribution is appropriately contextual: distinguishing participation forms and motives in remote physics laboratories. It does not claim to discover lurking or establish a causal mechanism. The advice about visible channels and unanswered posts follows specific findings on inaccessible communities and frustrating exchanges (pp. 10–12).

6. **Language and rhythm.** The prose is controlled, readable, and concrete where the argument needs examples: anticipating experimental difficulties, giving incorrect advice, and regretting isolation. “A less orderly pattern” is intelligible in context because the next sentence specifies the comparisons. The short interpretive statements give the discussion direction without reducing it to a sequence of methodological disclaimers. One sentence about unresolved difference size can be made more exact, but it is not an established factual error.

7. **Author voice and justified position.** The text takes a defensible position about recognizing engagement and understanding silence. It does not let the need for caution eliminate the educational conclusion. The argument remains an interpretation of the evidence, not a string of borrowed formulations or generic instructions.

8. **Qualification and redundancy.** The final paragraph's limitations serve distinct purposes: untested practices, unresolved causal direction, limited representativeness, and absence of demonstrated learning gains. They are proportionate to the claims being made. N also avoids the common error of interpreting nonsignificant outsider comparisons as equality. No blanket request for more hedging is warranted.

9. **Remaining author work.** There is no mandatory rewrite established by this assessment. If revising, clarify the precision of the outsider comparisons (N-O1). Before presenting the underlying statistical result as independently validated, reconcile the source reporting (V1). Do not add a lengthy caution paragraph to this already bounded discussion.

## R: assessment on the nine dimensions

1. **Facts and original meaning.** R preserves the original passage's central commitments: lurkers belong, lower reported self-efficacy deserves attention, some students prefer vicarious learning, all should have the opportunity to contribute, and intentional course design should support participation. It retains collaborative design projects, platform familiarity, staff preparation, smaller discussion groups, and evaluation of proposed approaches. Omitting the original class-size comparison and the claim that most community members did not contribute is reasonable compression within 200–260 words. “Reported self-efficacy” improves measurement precision over the original “demonstrated.” No outcome or intervention is invented.

2. **Educational problem and central judgment.** The revised opening preserves the tension between shared laboratory learning and legitimate participation without posting. “Not every student has to become a full-fledged contributor, but all should feel they may do so” retains the author's clear educational position. R therefore remains a revision of the supplied argument, rather than a replacement discussion with a different thesis.

3. **Reasoning and organization.** Two paragraphs separate the participation principle from design implications. The recommendations follow the identified problems: unfamiliar tools can impede participation, confidence and experience relate to behavior, and smaller groups might make contribution easier. The second paragraph is necessarily compressed but remains coherent, ending with evaluation rather than an unsupported effectiveness claim.

4. **Theory and literature within the reading boundary.** The original theoretical orientation and relevant citations are preserved. The prefatory attribution explicitly says the cited works were not independently read, and the small-group claim also uses a secondary citation. That is adequate transparency for this editing task. No mandatory requirement to repeat “as cited in” after every retained citation follows from the supplied request.

5. **Explanation and contribution.** The revision preserves the original intervention logic without pretending the current study tested collaborative projects or smaller groups. It usefully links staff preparation to recognizing student motivation and behavior. The task is a concise implications edit; it need not independently reconstruct the full empirical argument or introduce a new contribution.

6. **Language and rhythm.** “Shared learning process,” “unfamiliar tools may impede participation,” and “supporting students whose lack of confidence hinders contribution” make the original passage more direct. Some theoretical terms remain abstract, but they are germane to the author's framework. 7. **Author voice and justified position.** The author's normative commitment survives the compression. “Warrants attention” preserves concern without exaggerating urgency, and “Options include” appropriately introduces candidate course designs. 8. **Qualification and redundancy.** The final call to evaluate cognitive and affective outcomes marks the recommendations as proposals. “Can help” is framed as something earlier work suggests; it is not presented as an effect shown by this study. An explicit acknowledgment of the small self-efficacy effect would be useful if the passage circulates alone, but it is optional because the original passage is an implications section, the finding is correctly stated, and the full context already describes the small effect and unresolved causal direction.

9. **Remaining author work.** No mandatory prose correction is established. Optionally restore the magnitude qualification in the self-efficacy sentence (R-O1), especially for standalone use. Check the source statistics (V1) before treating them as verified. Retaining the current concise normative conclusion is warranted.

## Findings requiring attention

### V1 — source issue requiring verification; applies to N and R

**Manuscript passages:** N paragraph 3, “Contributors scored higher than lurkers in the reported post hoc comparison”; R paragraph 1, “their lower reported self-efficacy than contributors warrants attention.”

**Source locations and evidence:** new-evidence.md, PDF p. 7, says 272 students completed the survey and 22 were removed after the attention check; p. 8 and Table 2 on p. 9 nevertheless retain groups of 110, 117, and 45, totaling 272. The results give ANOVA statistics as “F(271)” (pp. 9–10), without the two degrees of freedom needed for the reported three-group ANOVA. Table 2 gives contributors' self-efficacy N = 110, SD = 9.96, mean = 45.45, and a 95% interval of 44.09–46.82. A conventional interval using that N and SD would have a half-width of approximately 1.88, whereas the printed interval's half-width is approximately 1.37. These are internal reporting questions, not proof that the substantive comparison is false.

**Why it matters:** The discussion and revision both draw instructional attention to a comparatively small, marginally significant self-efficacy result. The analyzed sample, uncertainty estimates, and inferential output should agree. The source pack warns that extraction can disturb tables, and the study's raw data and statistical output are unavailable.

**Action:** Verify the analyzed sample flow, original table layout/values, ANOVA degrees of freedom, and Tukey output against the original source or analysis records when access is authorized. Do not invent corrected figures or treat these discrepancies as a demonstrated error in N or R. Neither manuscript reproduces the questionable numerical details; both accurately describe the published comparison.

### N-O1 — optional precision improvement

**Passage:** N paragraph 3, “The latter comparisons leave the size of those differences unresolved.”

**Source:** new-evidence.md, PDF pp. 9–10, Table 2 and the report of nonsignificant outsider comparisons. The table supplies group means, while uncertainty remains about the population differences.

**Why it matters:** “Size ... unresolved” could be read as saying no estimates exist. The sentence's evident purpose—to avoid treating a nonsignificant test as equality—is sound.

**Optional action:** State that the comparisons do not establish equivalent self-efficacy, or specify that uncertainty remains about population differences. No change in the substantive conclusion is needed.

### R-O1 — optional magnitude context

**Passage:** R paragraph 1, “their lower reported self-efficacy than contributors warrants attention.”

**Source:** revision-original.md, first paragraph, makes the same concern; revision-context.md, PDF p. 14, explicitly says the effect size was small and causal direction unclear. The supplied findings also report a small effect (new-evidence.md, PDF pp. 9–10).

**Why it matters:** In standalone use, naming the small magnitude would help readers calibrate the strength of this concern. The existing wording does not claim a large difference or establish causation, so additional caution is not an accuracy requirement.

**Optional action:** Add a brief small-effect qualification if the passage is intended to stand alone; retain the author's concern and opportunity-to-participate principle.
