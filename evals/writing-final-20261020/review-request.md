# Independent manuscript assessment

Read the supplied source material and two anonymous manuscripts. `N.md` is a new 350–450-word discussion written from `new-evidence.md` only. `R.md` is a 200–260-word edit of `revision-original.md`, with `revision-context.md` available. These are different operations, not competing rule versions; do not rank N against R as though they were experimental arms. Attribution outside the manuscript body is excluded from the length request.

Assess each manuscript against its own evidence and request:

1. Facts and, for revision, the original author's meaning.
2. Educational problem and central judgment.
3. Reasoning and organization of evidence.
4. Theory and literature use within the actual reading boundary.
5. Explanation and contribution.
6. Natural, concrete language and rhythm.
7. Author voice and justified position.
8. Necessary qualification and redundant disclaimers.
9. Specific remaining changes needed from an author.

For each substantive concern identify the manuscript passage, the relevant source passage/page, why it matters, and whether it is a mandatory correction, a source issue requiring verification, or an optional preference. Do not assume a published passage is error-free. Do not treat a preference for more caution or stronger language as an accuracy criterion. Do not score writing by word/phrase frequency, sentence length, or an AI detector.

Explain what works as well as what does not. Avoid a numerical overall score. Save `assessment.md` and `findings.json`, with separate N/R decisions and mandatory/verification/optional findings. Record the actual files read and any evidence limitations in `read-log.json`.

Use only the assigned sources and anonymous manuscripts. Do not inspect the skill, development notes, writer rationale, self-review, other runs or other reviewers. Do not browse. Assess the supplied files; do not edit them.
