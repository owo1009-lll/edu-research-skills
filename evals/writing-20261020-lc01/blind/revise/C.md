In the observed Google Classroom activities at Highland School, literacy practices foregrounded typing and backgrounded speech: pupils largely entered their thoughts and ideas into online documents. Every pupil could contribute to each topic under discussion, but communication was delegated to these documents, with little verbal interaction outside them. This pattern echoes Colton's (2019, p. 432) account of technology opening participation while human and nonhuman elements guide learners. The shared documents set discussion parameters through preplanned visuals, so opportunities to contribute coexisted with an organization of classroom agency that favored those visuals.
