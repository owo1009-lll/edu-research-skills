No unrelated research problem is introduced.

3. **Reasoning and evidence organization.** The sequence is clear, with modest restatement. The paragraph proceeds from typing, to participation and delegation, to Colton, and finally agency. “Every pupil could contribute” and “therefore enabled participation” repeat the enabling side, while the last sentence also performs the necessary synthesis; this is a small economy issue rather than a reasoning defect.

4. **Theory and literature use, including secondary attribution.** The conceptual vocabulary is retained; the secondary source chain is less explicit than B's. “communication was delegated” and the human/nonhuman formulation preserve the sociomaterial explanation. “Colton's (2019, p. 432) account” reproduces the citation form in the original but does not identify Unsworth as the consulted intermediary within the body. The pack supports the paraphrase as Unsworth's reading, not independent verification of Colton. The withheld heading may resolve the framing.

5. **Explanation and concrete contribution.** Sufficient explanatory work for this compression. The final clause, “preplanned visuals that framed those discussions,” identifies what organizes participation. The manuscript keeps the concrete setting and communicative practice. It need not add the laptop/commenting episode or other source-pack scenes to fulfill this paragraph-level task.

6. **Naturalness, specificity and rhythm.** Natural and economical overall. The opening colon efficiently connects “typing over speech” to what pupils did. “the documents in which they typed” repeats information already available from the opening and could be tightened, but it is idiomatic and clear.

7. **Authorial voice, conceptual framework and justified strength.** Preserves the author's conceptual stance and appropriate strength. The retained terms “literacy practices,” “delegated” and “agency” keep the argument within its original framework. “balanced in favor” conveys the author's interpretation without changing it into total technological control or a claim that teachers have no agency.

8. **Necessary qualification and avoidable repetition.** “largely,” “little” and “could contribute” preserve nonabsolute description and participation opportunity. No general disclaimer is needed. Explicitly locating the claim in observed activities would be a useful optional refinement; participation is stated twice, as noted above.

9. **Concrete corrections an author would still need to make.** No substantive rewrite is required. If the surrounding attribution does not identify the indirect source chain, clarify that Colton is being paraphrased through Unsworth. ## Comparative judgment and remaining corrections

No defensible overall winner. All four are successful, closely comparable compressions. B has the clearest secondary-source trail; C most clearly bounds the observation; D gives the most immediately legible closing explanation. A remains coherent and faithful, with a little more restatement. These different advantages do not warrant a forced overall ranking.

**Overall comparison: A = B = C = D**, with different local strengths; the tie does not imply identical prose.

No demonstrated major factual error or loss of the central conceptual argument in any manuscript.

B explicitly identifies Colton as encountered through Unsworth. A, C and D retain the original paragraph's primary-style Colton citation. For a standalone secondary paraphrase, that should be made explicit; an accompanying heading may already establish the relevant source chain, so this is a conditional citation clarification rather than proof of false attribution or missing delivery.

C's “In the observed ... activities” provides the strongest local boundary. The contextual references to Google Classroom in A, B and D can reasonably be read as limiting “every pupil/student” to participants in those activities; the pack does not demonstrate that those formulations falsely include all school pupils. Explicit observation-level wording would nevertheless remove ambiguity.

The most useful remaining author checks are therefore conditional citation clarification for A/C/D, optional observation-level wording for A/B/D, and optional local economy edits, especially A's restatement and C's abstract final clause. B has no demonstrated citation correction to make. There is no basis to require extra source-pack scenes, measured participation evidence that the source does not provide, or a different paragraph structure.
