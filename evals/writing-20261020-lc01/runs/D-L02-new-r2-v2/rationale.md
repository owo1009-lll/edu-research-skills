# Drafting rationale

This is a new English findings/discussion subsection, written as an attributed secondary-writing exercise. The body must contain 350–450 words; the initial attribution heading is excluded. The assigned source pack is the sole empirical source. This rationale precedes the first draft.

The educational question is how shared cloud documents participate in making classroom practices. The supplied ethnographic episodes show teachers developing an activity through images, speech and simultaneous editing; a shared deck carrying agreements into adjacent classrooms; and pupils contributing through an online space whose preplanned visuals organize their responses. Read through Unsworth's combination of actor-network theory and literacy studies, these episodes support an account of agency formed through relations among people, documents, technologies and institutional arrangements. The contribution to foreground is that collaboration and standardization are produced through some of the same literacy practices. The subsection will follow documents from planning into instruction and participation, rather than summarize the entire study.

The setting and leadership expectations will establish the conditions of the account. Frances's positive account of showing ideas visually will remain distinct from the author's interpretation. Jamie and Meri's common instructional language and different pace will specify the extent of standardization. Laurie's episode will support the author's argument that participation can be opened while agency favors preplanned visuals. The closing claim will locate document influence within continued human participation. This is an interpretation of the supplied ethnographic account, not an estimate of educational effects.

Selection: omit resource blackboxing, report editing, evening work, burnout and post-pandemic extensions because each would need space to develop a different argument. Omit Colton because the original was not supplied and Unsworth's own conceptual account and observations suffice. Use no direct quotations: the selected material is paraphrased in the pack. No new themes, coding or independent fieldwork will be claimed.

The sequence will be: institutional expectations and shared planning; delegation into lessons; pupil participation and the direction of agency. Core terms are cloud documents, shared deck, agency, delegation, and actor-network theory with literacy studies. Terms will be explained through actions rather than accumulated as labels.
