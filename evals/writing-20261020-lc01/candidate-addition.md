## Example: use different actions to give a concept content

This newly written illustration draws on Fillmore, Phyak and Paudel's teacher profiles (2026, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11159-025-10217-7). It is an illustration, not a quotation or a paragraph template.

> Both Yogendra and Ganga Prasad were outsiders to their schools' Indigenous language communities, yet they used their authority differently. Yogendra built relationships and sought community approval for mother-tongue education; Ganga Prasad opposed its return and withheld information or support for related teacher training. The contrast makes community connection and accountability central to the authors' decolonial account of agency: outsider status alone cannot explain these different actions.

The actions do the explanatory work: seeking approval and restricting access to training give different content to exercising authority. The final judgment develops the authors' concept rather than adding a general claim that context matters. The example preserves their commitment to Indigenous language education; it does not turn agency into a neutral scale of personal initiative. The comparison is interpretive, not an estimate controlling all differences between schools. Use the relationships available in the actual material; a study of interaction or the movement of texts need not become a comparison of two people.
