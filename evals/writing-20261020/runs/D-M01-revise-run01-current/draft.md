Low-level solicitations can initiate sensemaking, but teachers need to push students experiencing difficulty to develop their ideas during discussion. Reliance on a single talk pattern is insufficient (Lowell et al., 2022), and overreliance on low-level solicitations prevents sensemaking (Carlone et al., 2011; Reinholz & Shah, 2018). Teachers should therefore scaffold extended contributions beyond the Initiation-Response-Evaluation sequence (Mehan, 1979) common in science classrooms (Nassaji & Wells, 2000; Pimentel & McNeill, 2013; Rees & Roth, 2019; Scott et al., 2006; Wells & Mejia Arauz, 2006). Such scaffolding can deepen sensemaking (Alexander, 2015; Resnick et al., 2010). Low expectations of these students' sensemaking can compromise their learning, given the important role of sensemaking in science content learning (Cannady et al., 2019).
