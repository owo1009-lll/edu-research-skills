Source: the supplied published paragraph by Juergensen and van Garderen (2025), PDF p. 18, first paragraph in the left column.

- Educational concern: students experiencing difficulty need opportunities to develop their thinking through classroom discussion.
- Starting point and limit: low-level solicitations can begin sensemaking, but teachers need to push students to do more with their ideas. A single talk pattern is insufficient (Lowell et al., 2022); overreliance on low-level solicitations prevents sensemaking (Carlone et al., 2011; Reinholz & Shah, 2018).
- Practical position: teachers need to scaffold extended contributions beyond a simple Initiation-Response-Evaluation sequence (Mehan, 1979). The paragraph identifies that sequence as common in science classrooms, supported by Nassaji and Wells (2000), Pimentel and McNeill (2013), Rees and Roth (2019), Scott et al. (2006), and Wells and Mejia Arauz (2006).
- Expected educational value: scaffolding can deepen sensemaking (Alexander, 2015; Resnick et al., 2010).
- Stakes: low expectations about the sensemaking of students experiencing difficulty can affect their learning, because sensemaking matters for science content learning (Cannady et al., 2019).
