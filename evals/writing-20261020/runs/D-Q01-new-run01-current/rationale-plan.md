# Writing rationale

This is an attributed secondary-writing demonstration of Högberg (2023), based exclusively on the supplied paraphrase. The educational question is whether pursuing achievement necessarily sacrifices wellbeing. The passage will explain the study’s addition of national comparisons to a debate often framed around individual differences, then derive a policy implication from the uneven evidence across outcomes and specifications.

Evidence supporting that argument: repeated cross-sectional comparisons with country/year fixed effects; weak and inconsistent adjusted national associations across belonging and the HBSC outcomes; instability of the initial affect associations; and the small negative school-belonging interaction. Evidence restricting it: the belonging interval includes both negative and positive associations, affect is available for one wave only, and the significant interaction prevents a blanket claim that every tested relationship is null. Neither competition mechanisms nor particular policies were directly tested.

The first paragraph will distinguish comparison levels and summarise the main national results. The second will retain the interaction as a bounded finding and develop the practical implication: assess particular educational arrangements against achievement and wellbeing together. Pursuing both is an explicit educational aim, not an intervention effect established by this study. Attribution will remain third-person throughout; no unexamined original literature will be cited.

Author attention: exact affect coefficients are omitted because the supplied pack reports discrepancies between the article and supplements. The pack also reports discrepancies in captions/model labels and an incomplete prose account of bootstrap findings; the identified table entries take precedence here. Original sources, supplementary D–I and underlying analyses have not been independently checked. This passage is a demonstration, not a report of our own empirical research.
