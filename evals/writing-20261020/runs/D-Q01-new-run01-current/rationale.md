# Writing rationale

The passage leads with Högberg’s contribution to the achievement–wellbeing debate: national comparisons provide weak and inconsistent support for either a general trade-off or a shared gain. It distinguishes individual positional advantage from national achievement, then compares the belonging estimates across model adjustments, the repeated wellbeing outcomes, and the less stable single-wave affect results. The belonging interval preserves uncertainty; the small interaction remains a bounded exception rather than becoming the main finding or a measured competitive mechanism.

The practical implication follows from national achievement being a broad proxy for diverse arrangements and from the different wellbeing outcomes: evaluate particular reforms against both aims and respond where harms are established. Pursuing both aims is an explicit educational commitment. The text does not present a tested intervention or guarantee that the aims can always be reconciled. Third-person attribution and the opening label identify the passage as secondary writing. No additional literature was cited because its original sources were not supplied.

Author attention: exact affect coefficients were omitted because the pack reports discrepancies between the article and supplements. It also reports caption/model-label discrepancies and omissions in the prose summary of bootstrap findings; the explicitly identified table entries govern this passage. Reuse beyond this exercise should check those discrepancies against the publication. Only the supplied paraphrase was read: the original article, cited originals and supplementary D–I were not independently examined, and no statistical analyses were recomputed.
