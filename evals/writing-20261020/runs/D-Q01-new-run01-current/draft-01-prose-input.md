# Discussion: secondary-writing demonstration based on Högberg (2023)

Högberg’s (2023) study adds a system-level test to a debate often informed by individual differences, finding weak and inconsistent support for either a general achievement–wellbeing trade-off or a shared gain. An individual’s relative academic advantage and a country’s higher mean achievement represent different comparisons; higher national averages alone reveal neither changing ranks nor the operation of competition. In repeated cross-sectional data, country and year fixed effects compare national changes over time rather than track individual development. The adjusted school-belonging coefficient was 0.069, with a 95% bootstrap interval spanning negative and positive associations [−0.053, 0.241]. Adjusted estimates for stress, psychosomatic complaints and life satisfaction likewise gave no consistent pattern. Initial adverse affect associations were sensitive to sample and covariate choices and became nonsignificant after full adjustment; these single-wave comparisons provide no evidence about affect trajectories.

The interaction results further qualify the idea that individual achievement and system achievement are interchangeable indicators of educational benefit. Higher national achievement was associated with a smaller individual-achievement slope for school belonging (interaction = −0.017), a small significant exception among three tested interactions, rather than evidence of a general competitive mechanism. Taken together, these findings favour examining particular educational arrangements. If achievement and wellbeing are both educational aims, policymakers should assess concrete reforms against both, retain arrangements that support both goals and respond when a specific practice harms wellbeing. Nursing or counselling may be considered as supportive responses, as Högberg proposes, but their effectiveness was not evaluated here. This shifts policy attention from assurances about national averages to the conditions under which particular practices serve pupils.
