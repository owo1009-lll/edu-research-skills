# Writing rationale

This is an attributed secondary-writing demonstration of Williamson et al. (2019), based solely on the supplied source pack. The subject is evidence generation in health and related social policy; it is not an education intervention.

The argument planned before drafting is: shared aspirations for impact leave the practical meaning of usefulness unsettled; differences within and between participant roles make negotiation of mutual benefit central. Distinguishing project initiation from collaboration across research stages then supports a practical implication: agree and revisit involvement where contextual knowledge is needed, with attention to partners' capacity.

The evidence consists of experienced participants' interview accounts across projects and the original authors' synthesis, as paraphrased in the pack. It supports an interpretation of differing experiences and a grounded practical recommendation, not a verified causal sequence or a ranking of partnership models by measured success.

The two paragraphs will connect interpretation to action. They will retain counterexamples: policy-makers also valued innovation, researchers also valued relational returns from small projects, and evidence reviews sometimes required contextual knowledge. Participant quotations will be paraphrased to avoid suggesting paired accounts or a verified sequence. No additional literature or reference list will be introduced.

Author attention: the source pack, not the original article or interview data, was inspected for this exercise. The proposed arrangements have not been tested against independent quality or policy-impact measures. Any extension beyond comparable research-policy work would need its own evidence.
