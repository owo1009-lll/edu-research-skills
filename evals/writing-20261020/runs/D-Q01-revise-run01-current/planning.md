# Editing rationale

This is an attributed adaptation of Högberg (2023), *Is There a trade-off Between Achievement and Wellbeing in Education Systems" New cross-country Evidence*, DOI 10.1007/s12187-023-10047-9, licensed CC BY 4.0 according to the supplied materials. The source paragraph is in Conclusion and Implications, first paragraph after the opening summary, locator B092. This is an editing exercise, not an original study or a submission.

## Argument reconstructed before drafting

The author moves from the policy relevance of within-country fixed effects comparisons, through the predominantly null results, to the distinction between average linear relationships and basic eligibility thresholds, then to a dual policy priority and compensation for policy-specific harm. The normative premise is that achievement and wellbeing are both valued. Preserve this sequence, the justified "should", the threshold hypothesis, Moilanen (2010), and the examples of school nursing and counselling.

The source pack supports weak and inconsistent evidence for either a general trade-off or a general gain. The intervals include adverse and beneficial associations, and the study provides neither equivalence tests nor policy intervention comparisons. Thus the particular inference that higher achievement makes little difference or poses no harm needs correction, not a replacement of the author's policy position.

## Planned local changes

| Unit | Source and argument | Editing decision |
|---|---|---|
| Policy relevance | Original opening; source pack, Data and comparisons | Retain within-country fixed effects comparisons and national/subnational policy scope; compress the opening. |
| Null findings | Original sentences 2-3; source pack, Results available for discussion and Source status | Correct inference from null findings to no difference; describe uncertain associations rather than established policy effects. Category (a): inference beyond design and precision. |
| Threshold distinction | Original middle sentences; source pack, Author's interpretation | Keep average linear relationships distinct from possible basic eligibility threshold benefits, basic skills, and school failure. Keep Moilanen (2010). |
| Policy action | Original final two sentences; source pack, Author's interpretation | Preserve the conditional value premise and priority to identify and implement policies supporting both outcomes, plus compensation for specific harms. Keep measures as recommendations. |

Terminology: use "achievement", "wellbeing", "fixed effects models", "basic eligibility thresholds", "school nursing" and "counselling" consistently. No abbreviations are needed.
