Within-country comparisons from the fixed effects models are especially relevant to policymakers because education policy is largely national or subnational in scope. The predominantly null findings provide weak support for either a general trade-off or a general gain in wellbeing as national achievement rises; uncertainty leaves both adverse and beneficial associations open. Policymakers should therefore neither assume that wellbeing must be sacrificed nor expect higher achievement alone to deliver substantial wellbeing gains. These linear models concern average achievement and average wellbeing; possible benefits associated with crossing basic eligibility thresholds remain untested in this study. Raising basic skills and preventing school failure may be especially beneficial for wellbeing (Moilanen, 2010). Although the study does not identify which specific policies involve trade-offs, identifying and implementing policies that support both achievement and wellbeing should be a priority where both outcomes are valued. Where particular policies harm pupils' wellbeing, policymakers should seek to counter that harm through compensatory measures such as investment in school nursing and counselling.
