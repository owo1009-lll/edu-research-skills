# Secondary-writing demonstration based on Högberg (2023)

Högberg (2023) adds national comparisons to a debate often informed by individual differences, finding weak and inconsistent support for either a general achievement–wellbeing trade-off or a shared gain. Individual achievement relative to peers and higher national achievement pose different questions: an individual’s positional advantage need not translate into collective benefit. Repeated cross-sectional comparisons with country and year fixed effects examine variation within countries over time. For school belonging, a near-zero unadjusted association became positive after fixed effects and socioeconomic adjustment (standardised coefficient = 0.069), but its 95% wild-cluster-bootstrap interval [−0.053, 0.241] includes negative and positive associations. Adjusted associations with stress, psychosomatic complaints and life satisfaction likewise offered weak support for either position. Initial adverse affect associations varied with samples and covariates and were nonsignificant after full adjustment; these single-wave comparisons address differences between countries, not changes over time.

The interaction analysis adds a qualification: the individual-achievement slope for school belonging was smaller at higher national achievement (interaction = −0.017). This small significant exception among three tested interactions is specific to belonging; the competitive mechanisms remain unmeasured. Together, these findings redirect attention to particular educational arrangements, for which national achievement is only a broad proxy. Given the educational commitment to both achievement and wellbeing, policymakers should assess concrete reforms against both aims and examine wellbeing outcomes separately, since belonging, feelings and stress capture different experiences. Arrangements that support both goals deserve pursuit; evidence that a particular practice harms wellbeing should prompt changes to that practice and support for affected pupils. The study grounds this agenda without establishing which measures will achieve it.
