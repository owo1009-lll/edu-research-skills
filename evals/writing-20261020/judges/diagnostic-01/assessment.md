## X: new discussion writing

**Overall:** X delivers a coherent, evidence-grounded discussion with a practical implication. Its 243 words and two paragraphs meet the 230–280-word/two-paragraph requirement. I find no demonstrated factual error or material departure from the requested argument. A more concrete bridge from epistemic authority to classroom practice is an optional improvement.

1. **Factual/author fidelity.** P1 S2 correctly says teacher-initiated sequences formed “a larger share of the recorded sequences.” The source table supports that comparison (29/52 versus 109/375); X does not convert sequences into numbers of students or individual participation rates. “Identified by teachers” preserves the classification's status. P1 S3 frames fear of error and classroom pacing within “teachers' perspectives”; P2 S4 attributes the open-ended response practice to “One teacher.” These are appropriately scoped claims. Omitting the disputed inferential statistics avoids reproducing an unresolved reporting problem.

2. **Question and core judgment.** The opening distinction between “access to science talk” and opportunities “to develop their ideas” directly answers what the two evidence strands add. P1's final sentence turns this into a judgment about what happens after an invitation to speak. The practical implication follows in P2 and explicitly retains equitable learning opportunities. This is an interpretive answer, not merely a summary of methods and findings.

3. **Reasoning/evidence organisation.** P1 moves from the observed solicitation pattern to teachers' accounts of access barriers and encouragement, then asks how contributions are used. P2 gives that question a conceptual interpretation and translates it into processing time and elaboration. “Interviews informed by these observations” accurately represents the phase connection without claiming a complete student-level explanatory linkage. The evidence supports examining these practices; X does not assert that a tested intervention improved participation.

4. **Theory/literature use.** “Epistemic authority, or whose knowledge is treated as authoritative” draws directly on the supplied article discussion and supplies a usable definition. The source attribution in the opening is sufficient for this secondary-writing passage; introducing unverified external literature would not be an improvement. The concept is used to interpret uptake of contributions, rather than appearing as an unexplained label.

5. **Explanation/contribution.** “Whether contributions receive approval and whether they become resources for shared reasoning” identifies the substantive issue: encouragement and development of ideas are related but separately examinable. The recommendation to “pair reassurance” with explanation avoids treating confirmation as inherently incompatible with sensemaking. An optional refinement would specify how classmates could examine and build on an idea, making the distribution of epistemic authority more concrete. That would deepen an existing connection, not repair an absent argument.

6. **Natural, specific prose and rhythm.** “Fear of error and faster classmates,” “protect processing time,” and “invite elaboration before judging correctness” provide specific classroom content. The paragraphs have a readable progression from interpretation to action. “This connection places participation within classroom relationships and pacing” (P1 S4) is more abstract than its surrounding examples, but its referent is recoverable and the sentence has a useful boundary-setting function. Recasting that phrase is a stylistic option, not a defect established by frequency or sentence length.

7. **Author voice.** X preserves the supplied authors' equitable-learning stance and non-fixed description of difficulty while maintaining a measured discussion voice. Its explicit attribution avoids presenting the study as the writer's own. Whether it reproduces the authors' individual stylistic voice cannot be established from this source pack and one separate paragraph; the new-writing request does not demand close stylistic imitation.

8. **Necessary qualification versus repetitive disclaimers.** “Leaving individual students' reasons for speaking unresolved” limits the explanatory reach of teacher accounts. “Confidence and learning gains were not measured” separately limits outcome claims. They do different work and are not repetitive. Neither prevents the passage from stating a clear practical position. The second could be integrated more tightly into its neighbouring sentences, but deletion is not needed for natural prose.

9. **Concrete author corrections.** No mandatory manuscript correction is established. If revising for greater conceptual specificity, connect P2's elaboration recommendation to classmates considering and developing one another's ideas. Preserve the distinction between a proposed opportunity and a demonstrated benefit. The separately requested writing-choice/author-attention note was not among the permitted files, so its delivery and adequacy are not assessable here.

## Y: compression of the published paragraph

**Overall:** Y provides a faithful, readable compression in one paragraph of 138 words, within the 125–160-word request. It preserves the author's practical position, distinct concepts and citation-to-claim relationships. Its strongest causal formulation is inherited from the original and remains an unresolved evidence check, not an identified new error.

1. **Factual/author fidelity.** “Low-level solicitations can be a useful starting point” preserves the original's qualified acceptance of an entry point. The passage then retains the need to develop ideas, avoid overreliance on one pattern, extend contributions beyond simple IRE, and maintain expectations for students experiencing difficulty. “Can compromise their learning” preserves the original concern about low expectations without introducing a measured effect size. The supplied record establishes faithful editing; it does not independently validate the underlying literature claims.

2. **Question and core judgment.** The central judgment remains that teachers should move students beyond low-level contributions through scaffolding because expectations and opportunities for sensemaking matter for learning. This meets the request to compress and improve progression without substituting a different argument. The source pack's epistemic-authority discussion need not be added to a paragraph whose original argument did not explicitly develop it.

3. **Reasoning/evidence organisation.** S1 establishes the starting-point/development distinction. S2 groups two reasons for avoiding restrictive patterns. “Teachers should therefore scaffold” (S3) supplies the practical response, S4 identifies its purpose, and S5 supplies the learning rationale. Moving the warning about overreliance ahead of the recommendation improves progression. It also preserves the distinction between solicitation demand and the IRE interaction sequence rather than treating them as synonyms.

4. **Theory/literature use.** All citation groups in the original remain attached to the corresponding retained claims: Lowell for single-pattern reliance; Carlone/Reinholz and Shah for low-level solicitation; Mehan for IRE; the five-source cluster for its prevalence in science classrooms; Alexander/Resnick for deeper sensemaking; Cannady for content learning. No new source is introduced. Citation presence and placement are assessable; whether those publications substantiate these formulations is not, because their full texts were not supplied or read.

5. **Explanation/contribution.** The revised paragraph retains the original's instructional logic rather than adding a new empirical contribution. “Such scaffolding can help students develop their ideas through deeper sensemaking” connects the recommendation to its purpose. The paragraph does not detail a classroom mechanism or strategy, but adding one is not required by this compression request and could import an argument absent from the original.

6. **Natural, specific prose and rhythm.** Y removes the original's repeated “push,” the awkward possessive in “students experiencing difficulties’ thinking,” and the long “Furthermore, when” construction. Its declarative sentences are easier to follow. S3 remains interrupted by a substantial citation cluster, but that density follows the express instruction to preserve citations supporting retained claims. It is a tradeoff, not sufficient reason to delete sources or diagnose unnatural prose.

7. **Author voice.** The directive “teachers need to push” and “Teachers should therefore scaffold” retain the original's active instructional stance. The ending preserves its concern about low expectations. Compression has not turned a practical argument into a neutral description or a methodological disclaimer. Full stylistic fidelity beyond this paragraph is not assessable.

8. **Necessary qualification versus repetitive disclaimers.** “Useful starting point” and “can help” retain relevant qualifications; no extraneous caveats have been added. The categorical “overreliance on low-level solicitations prevents sensemaking” (S2) restates the original's “does not allow for sensemaking to take place.” The pack neither verifies nor disproves that strength of claim. It should be recorded as an inherited evidence question, not blamed on the revision or automatically weakened. A starting point and subsequent overreliance are different conditions, so the two sentences are not inherently contradictory.

9. **Concrete author corrections.** No new mandatory correction is established. Before reuse as a substantive literature claim, the author could check whether the cited sources warrant “prevents”; if they support restriction of opportunity rather than impossibility, “can constrain opportunities for sensemaking” would be a conditional correction. Retain the present form if that stronger claim is adequately supported and intended. This is not a required change to complete the supplied editing exercise. The separate explanation and adaptation attribution record were not supplied for inspection; their adequacy cannot be judged.
