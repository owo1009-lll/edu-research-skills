## X: assessment against new-request.md

1. **Factual and author fidelity — sound; severity: none.** The opening identifies Högberg and the task's secondary status (X:1, 3). The belonging estimate of 0.069 and interval [−0.053, 0.241] match source-pack:23; the smaller individual-achievement slope and −0.017 interaction match source-pack:25. The affect summary preserves the distinction between initial adverse associations and nonsignificant fully adjusted associations (source-pack:21). The policy emphasis on assessing particular arrangements follows source-pack:31–33. No additional empirical findings are invented.

2. **Problem and core judgment — clear; severity: none.** The first sentence states what the study contributes: national comparisons and weak, inconsistent support for either a general trade-off or a shared gain (X:3; source-pack:7, 27, 31). The next sentence explains why individual advantage and collective improvement are distinct questions. This gives the passage an argument rather than only a results inventory.

3. **Reasoning and evidence organisation — coherent; severity: none.** X:3 moves from the debate to within-country comparisons and their results, then identifies affect as a single-wave comparison between countries. X:5 introduces the interaction as a qualification and uses the mixed evidence to motivate attention to particular practices. The interval is interpreted as allowing both negative and positive associations, rather than as proof of no effect. The text does not claim that repeated cross-sections follow the same pupils or that fixed effects establish policy treatment effects. Naming HBSC and its different pupils would add methodological detail but is not necessary to the argument or short-discussion brief.

4. **Theory and literature use — proportionate and attributable; severity: none.** The positional-advantage distinction is faithful to the theoretical rationale in source-pack:9. The later statement that competitive mechanisms remain unmeasured prevents the theory from being presented as a demonstrated explanation (X:5). The text neither invents a direct test of competition nor claims to have independently read the underlying literature. It is not required to reproduce every theoretical alternative or the threshold discussion in this new-writing task.

5. **Explanation and contribution — developed adequately; severity: none.** The contribution is not just an additional national coefficient: it is the distinction between levels of comparison, heterogeneity across wellbeing outcomes and the limited reach of national achievement as a proxy (X:3, 5; source-pack:14–17, 31). Assessing concrete reforms against both aims and examining wellbeing outcomes separately follows from those distinctions. Changes to a harmful practice and support for affected pupils are clearly policy recommendations, not interventions claimed to have been evaluated.

6. **Natural, concrete prose and rhythm — effective, with optional light editing; severity: low for style only.** The first paragraph is necessarily denser because it combines several results and model distinctions. Its sentences nevertheless have identifiable subjects and clear transitions. “Belonging, feelings and stress capture different experiences” makes the second paragraph more concrete (X:5). An optional improvement would replace “a shared gain” (X:3) with “gains in both achievement and wellbeing” for immediate clarity. This is a preference, not a substantive correction; the numerical detail is not inherently excessive.

7. **Author voice — appropriate to secondary writing; severity: none.** X names the study's author and uses third-person attribution rather than pretending these are the writer's data. It retains the supplied author's interest in pursuing both outcomes while evaluating particular policies (X:5; source-pack:31–33). This task does not require imitation of the original paragraph's wording or rhythm. The author's preferences beyond the supplied material cannot be assessed.

8. **Necessary qualifications versus repetitive disclaimers — balanced; severity: none.** The belonging interval, cross-sectional affect distinction, outcome-specific interaction and unmeasured mechanisms address different inferential risks. The closing sentence also distinguishes a defensible policy agenda from evidence that any particular measure works. These qualifications are integrated into the argument; their number alone is not a quality defect. More caveats about every possible confounder would be unnecessary for the brief.

9. **Specific corrections needed from the author — none established in the body; deliverable check remains.** Verify that the requested short separate note explains the comparison choices and any relevant source checks (new-request:5). If absent, add it outside the two paragraphs. The source pack reports discrepancies in exact affect coefficients and some labels (source-pack:39); X avoids the unverified exact affect figures, so the passage itself requires no numerical repair on that basis. A proportionate note can explain this choice without undertaking a full audit.

## Y: assessment against revise-request.md

1. **Factual and author fidelity — sound; severity: none.** Y:1 preserves the policy relevance of within-country comparisons; the distinction between average linear relationships and possible eligibility-threshold benefits; the citation to Moilanen (2010); the priority of policies supporting both outcomes; and nursing and counselling as compensatory examples (original:5). Compression removes some repetition and one additional explanation of policymakers' national scope without changing the substantive position. The examples are not presented as evaluated interventions.

2. **Problem and core judgment — preserved and appropriately corrected; severity: none.** The original moves too readily from predominantly null results to reassurance that achievement need not harm wellbeing (original:5; source-pack:37). Y retains the argument against an assumed inevitable sacrifice and against expecting broad wellbeing gains, while stating that uncertainty leaves adverse and beneficial associations open (Y:1, sentences 2–3). This repairs the inference without replacing the author's policy argument.

3. **Reasoning and evidence organisation — strong progression; severity: none.** The sequence is policy relevance, interpretation of the average findings, the untested threshold distinction, and action on specific policies. “These linear models” links the threshold discussion to the preceding findings; “Although the study does not identify” introduces the policy recommendation's evidential boundary. No numerical estimates are needed to accomplish this requested compression.

4. **Theory and literature use — faithful within the supplied limits; severity: none.** The distinction between average achievement and crossing basic eligibility thresholds is retained, and the possible benefit of raising basic skills remains qualified by “may” (Y:1, sentences 4–5; original:5). Moilanen (2010) is preserved as requested. Its independent evidential support cannot be checked from the supplied files; that unresolved source question belongs in a separate note, not in an invented correction or deletion of the citation.

5. **Explanation and contribution — appropriate to revision; severity: none.** This paragraph's contribution is a clearer version of the existing policy argument, not a new synthesis of every result. It explains why average findings cannot settle threshold questions or identify particular policies, then retains the positive agenda of supporting both outcomes and countering harms. Omitting the interaction and the full affect results is appropriate to this task's scope.

6. **Natural, concrete prose and rhythm — effective; severity: none.** The paragraph reads as connected academic prose. “Raising basic skills and preventing school failure” and “school nursing and counselling” provide concrete anchors. The opening is more direct than the original, and the threshold passage no longer relies on consecutive “however” transitions. The uncertainty clause is explicit but does not dominate the paragraph. No stylistic revision is necessary to meet the brief.

7. **Author voice — preserved in substance; severity: none.** “Should be a priority where both outcomes are valued” and “policymakers should seek to counter that harm” retain the original's conditional, normative policy voice. The revision does not import the positional-competition argument or a new programme of statistical criticism. The shift from reassurance to qualified interpretation is authorised by the request to correct unsupported inferences. Whether the surrounding record identifies this as an adaptation of Högberg cannot be determined from Y.md alone (original:3).

8. **Necessary qualifications versus repetitive disclaimers — well judged; severity: none.** The uncertainty statement, untested thresholds and inability to identify specific policy trade-offs each concern a distinct claim. The paragraph continues to make substantive recommendations. “Predominantly null” is not used as proof of equivalence or absence because the following clause explicitly leaves both directions open (Y:1, sentence 2). Neither removing that qualification nor adding a catalogue of confounders would improve fidelity to the brief.

9. **Specific corrections needed from the author — none established in the body; record checks remain.** Verify the separate explanation of the changed null-result inference and the unresolved Moilanen source check (revise-request:5). Ensure the accompanying record explicitly labels the paragraph an adaptation of Högberg (2023), as original:3 requires. These matters cannot be declared omitted from the complete delivery on the basis of the manuscript file alone.
