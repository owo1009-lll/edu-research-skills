# Discussion

Conventional cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept cross-lagged panel models (RI-CLPMs) are sometimes portrayed as rivals, with RI-CLPMs assumed to be universally preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We regard them as complementary perspectives on how academic self-concept (students’ competence beliefs) and achievement relate over time. CLPMs compare students’ relative standing, accounting for earlier measurements but leaving stable differences and within-person fluctuations intertwined. RI-CLPMs separate these components, examining whether deviations from a student’s usual self-concept predict later deviations in achievement, and vice versa. Associations across students, often studied in cross-sectional research, are frequently interpreted as causal processes within students without a supporting design or analysis. Between-person and within-person relationships are likely to coincide only under restrictive conditions, including stable parameters over time and similar relationships across individuals, which are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue that research should examine the commonalities and differences between these complementary perspectives.
