# Attributed adaptation of Marsh, Pekrun, and Lüdtke (2022)

Although CLPMs and RI-CLPMs are sometimes presented as rivals, with the latter assumed to be preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021), we regard them as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept and achievement. Conventional cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) examine students’ relative standing over time without separating stable differences between students from fluctuations within them. Random-intercept cross-lagged panel models (RI-CLPMs) separate these components and examine whether deviations from a student’s usual self-concept predict later deviations in achievement, and vice versa. The distinction matters because associations across students, commonly examined in cross-sectional research, are often interpreted as causal processes within students without an appropriate design or analysis. Between-person and within-person relationships are likely to coincide only under restrictive conditions, including stable parameters over time and similar relationships across individuals, which are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue for considering what these perspectives share alongside their differences, rather than treating them as competing research paradigms.
