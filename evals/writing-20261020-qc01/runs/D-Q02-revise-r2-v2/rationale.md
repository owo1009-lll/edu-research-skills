## Argument map prepared before drafting

The authors contest the presentation of CLPMs and RI-CLPMs as antagonistic approaches or of the latter as universally preferable. Their positive claim is complementarity: both provide substantive information about academic self-concept and achievement over time. They distinguish comparisons across individuals from relationships within individuals. They object to causal interpretations unsupported by research design or analysis and explain why equivalence between comparison levels requires restrictive conditions. Their final recommendation, following Voelkle et al. (2014), is to examine commonalities together with differences. Orth et al. (2021) supplies the original qualification to the portrayal of the methodological debate.

The source pack clarifies the comparison: CLPM lagged paths concern relative standing conditional on earlier measurements and do not decompose stable differences and within-person variation; RI-CLPM lagged paths concern deviations from a student's usual level. The latter are common parameters across students, not separate causal estimates for each student. This distinction supports the authors' conceptual position and provides the minimum explanation needed for readers unfamiliar with the model families.
