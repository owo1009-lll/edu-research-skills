# Writing rationale

This plan was saved before drafting. The task is a new, attributed English discussion passage of 450–550 body words, using only the supplied source pack. It is a single passage, so the skill's full-manuscript confirmation, evidence tables, terminology tables, consistency checker and Word conversion do not apply.

The educational question is whether mathematics self-concept should be understood only as a reflection of achievement, or also as a predictor of subsequent achievement. The available evidence reports positive reciprocal lagged associations with both grades and standardized test scores across Grades 5–9. The distinctive contribution is that this directional pattern appears under two different comparisons: students' relative standing with earlier measurements controlled, and deviations from a student's usual level after stable differences are separated. The argument will preserve the authors' complementary interpretation while explaining these comparisons in ordinary language.

Evidence supporting this judgement: annual observations of 3,425 Bavarian students; two distinct achievement indicators; agreement in the directional pattern across model families and the reported lag-2 and measured-covariate extensions. Improved CLPM fit after adding lag-2 paths, alongside comparatively stable focal paths, supplies a concrete reason to distinguish model fit from substantive conclusions. These claims come from the pack's Design, Results and Authors' conceptual position sections.

Evidence limiting the judgement: observational assignment; common within-person parameters rather than individual causal effects; weak empirical testing of confounding because the measured covariates changed little; unmeasured time-varying confounding; incomplete participation and assumptions behind full-information maximum likelihood; no measurement of the proposed feedback–attribution–effort process; no intervention test. These limits will inform the research argument, with the mechanism qualified at its point of use and general design issues grouped together. No numerical effect sizes or implied educational benefit magnitudes will be supplied.

The planned movement is findings and the two achievement indicators → concrete explanation of model comparisons and their agreement → model fit and interpretation → possible feedback process and remaining design questions → attention to both beliefs and performance, culminating in the authors' factorial trial proposal. Paragraph divisions will follow readability rather than a fixed template. Prior studies cited by the article, including Haney and Durlak, will not be treated as independently verified sources. Source discrepancies and the unread supplement belong in the separate note, not in the manuscript's argument.

Attribution will sit in a heading outside the manuscript word count. The body will refer to the published study without claiming ownership. The source article and its tables have not been independently read or reanalysed for this task. Only the provided pack establishes the factual basis.

The saved first draft followed this plan in five paragraphs. After the actual checker runs and AI self-review, the complete revision clarified the opening definition, added body attribution, explained complementarity more directly, and replaced an unnecessary explained-variance aside with greater emphasis on educational attention and process measurement. The revised body was initially too short; this was detected and recorded rather than hidden. A small follow-up clarified the annual within-person comparison and common parameter, made the stability of findings under both extensions explicit, and grounded educators' attention to school grades in their role as visible feedback. Drafts 01, 02 and 03 remain intact; final.md is the delivery copy of draft 03.
