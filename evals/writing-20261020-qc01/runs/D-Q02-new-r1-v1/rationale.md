# Writing rationale

## Plan recorded before the first draft

This is a new, attributed English discussion passage of 450–550 words, using only the supplied source pack. It is a single-section task, so the skill's full-manuscript approval, consistency checker, reference-list and Word-delivery routes do not apply. The request explicitly permits Markdown only and excludes a reference list and tables from the deliverable.

The argument will address whether mathematics self-concept deserves attention alongside achievement. The study supplies positive reciprocal longitudinal associations across grades and standardized tests, under models answering different questions. Its central contribution is to locate the reciprocal pattern in both relative-standing comparisons and deviations from students' usual levels, while retaining the distinction between the estimands. The educational judgement is that self-beliefs and performance both merit attention; the proposed benefit of combining interventions remains a prediction to test.

Supporting evidence: repeated measurements from Grades 5 to 9; two achievement indicators with different educational meanings; reciprocal patterns in both model families; persistence under reported lag-2 and covariate extensions. Evidence limiting interpretation: observational design and unmeasured time-varying confounding; weak resistance-to-confounding test because the measured covariates had little influence; unmeasured psychological process; incomplete participation across waves; no tested intervention combination or verified coefficient vectors. These constraints will inform the passage's argument rather than become repeated generic cautions.

The passage will use approximately five connected paragraphs: substantive contribution; accessible model interpretation; what specification comparisons establish; process explanation and inferential limits; educational action and the proposed factorial experiment. The scope is Bavarian secondary students over annual intervals. No source beyond the pack will be presented as independently read.
