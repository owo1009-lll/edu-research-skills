## Substantive review and revision decisions

1. **Contribution and opening:** The finding, sample, timing and two indicators match the pack. The discussion explains why grades and standardized tests both matter. “Assign predictive priority exclusively” is unnecessarily abstract; replace it with a direct contrast to a one-directional account. Do not claim a first discovery or superiority over independently reviewed literature.
2. **Model meanings:** The CLPM correctly retains both stable person differences and within-person variation. The RI-CLPM description should specify deviations from the usual level over the study and a common estimated within-person association. Replace “separate effect for each individual” to avoid letting causal or individual-effect language enter a sentence intended to exclude that interpretation. Explain the positive direction for both constructs plainly.
3. **Specification comparison:** The fit improvement and relative stability of the focal paths match the pack. Keep the argument that fit, parameter meaning and the substantive question jointly guide evaluation. The explained-variance warning is legitimate, but it should serve that positive model-evaluation point rather than form a free-standing prohibition.
4. **Evidence boundaries:** The candidate feedback–beliefs–effort account is identified as plausible and unmeasured. The source supports the missingness concern and use of full-information maximum likelihood. Group general observational/confounding limits here, while preserving the result-specific explanation of why covariate stability is a weak test. Do not imply that repeated observations verify missing-data assumptions.
5. **Educational implication:** The first draft's “broader basis for deciding what support to investigate” is vague. State the supported educational priority more concretely: consider students' competence beliefs alongside attainment when deciding where support may be needed. This expresses the authors' position, not a validated screening procedure or intervention effect.
6. **Research implication:** The factorial trial correctly includes the combined programme, each component and neither. The final “would establish” sentence promises too much about connecting the observational association to a programme. Replace it with the trial's actual question: whether combining the components provides benefits beyond either component. The predicted advantage remains untested.
