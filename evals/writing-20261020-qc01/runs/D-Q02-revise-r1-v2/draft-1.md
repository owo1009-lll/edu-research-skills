# Adapted from Marsh, Pekrun, and Lüdtke (2022)

Conventional cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept CLPMs (RI-CLPMs) are often presented as competing approaches, with RI-CLPMs assumed to be inherently preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We see these models as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept and achievement over time. CLPMs compare students’ relative standing without separating stable differences between students from variation within students; RI-CLPMs examine departures from each student’s usual level. These distinct comparisons provide useful substantive information, with different strengths and weaknesses. Most educational and psychological research compares people at one time point, yet causal interpretations often exceed what the design supports. Relationships between people need not describe relationships within a person. Equivalence is plausible only under restrictive conditions, such as stable parameters over time and similar relationships across individuals, that are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue that researchers should consider commonalities alongside differences between these perspectives when examining academic self-concept and achievement over time.
