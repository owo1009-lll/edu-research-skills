# Discussion adaptation — Marsh, Pekrun, and Lüdtke (2022)

Cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept cross-lagged panel models (RI-CLPMs) are sometimes presented as rivals, with the latter assumed to be universally preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We regard them as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept and achievement over time. CLPMs compare students’ relative standing while adjusting for preceding measurements, without separating stable differences between students from within-student variation. RI-CLPMs separate these components and examine whether deviations from students’ usual levels predict subsequent deviations. Their distinct comparisons provide useful information, but neither perspective alone warrants a causal interpretation. Much educational and psychological research examines differences between people, yet its findings are often interpreted causally beyond what the design supports. Between-person and within-person relations are likely to coincide only under restrictive conditions, including stable parameters over time and homogeneous relations across individuals; these conditions are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue for examining the approaches’ commonalities alongside differences rather than treating them as competing paradigms.
