## Scope and pre-draft argument map

This is an attributed, one-paragraph editing exercise in English, using only `original.md` and `source-pack.md`. The requested manuscript body is 140–170 words. The source is Marsh, Pekrun, and Lüdtke (2022), *Directional Ordering of Self-Concept, School Grades, and Standardized Tests Over Five Years*, Discussion, “The Juxtaposition of Results Based On CLPMs and RI-CLPMs,” opening paragraph (B132). The output is an adaptation of their published paragraph, licensed [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); the [article link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-022-09662-9) is supplied attribution, not a source opened during this task.

The original argument is: methodological debate sometimes treats CLPMs and RI-CLPMs as rivals and privileges the latter → both illuminate longitudinal relations between academic self-concept and achievement → their comparisons differ → research across persons is often interpreted causally without an adequate design → equivalence of relations between and within persons depends on restrictive conditions → researchers should consider commonalities together with differences. The authors’ central contribution in this paragraph is the complementary interpretation of distinct comparisons, not a claim that the models are equivalent or that either identifies causality.

Support for the argument comes from the original paragraph’s conceptual comparison and the source pack’s definitions of the models. The pack also reports agreement in reciprocal patterns across model families, but adding those findings would displace the paragraph’s existing argument. The key boundary is that distinct comparisons should not be equated: the CLPM leaves stable person differences and within-person variation undecomposed, whereas the RI-CLPM separates them. The original’s stationarity and homogeneity conditions must remain. Its causal caution is a substantive part of the comparison, not a generic disclaimer to be automatically deleted.
