# Discussion adaptation — Marsh, Pekrun, and Lüdtke (2022)

Cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept cross-lagged panel models (RI-CLPMs) are sometimes presented as rivals, with RI-CLPMs assumed to be universally preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We regard both as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept and achievement over time. CLPMs compare students’ relative standing conditional on preceding measurements, leaving stable differences between students and within-student variation combined. RI-CLPMs separate these components and examine whether deviations from students’ usual levels predict later deviations. These comparisons answer different questions about longitudinal relations. Most educational and psychological research is cross-sectional, yet associations between people are often interpreted causally beyond what the design supports. Between-person and within-person relations are likely to coincide only under restrictive conditions, including stable parameters over time and homogeneous relations across individuals; these conditions are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue for examining the perspectives’ commonalities alongside their differences rather than treating them as competing paradigms.
