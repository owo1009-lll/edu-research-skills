
## Making a comparison concrete

When a reader needs the comparison to understand the finding, explain what the compared students, occasions or conditions actually differ in, then show why that distinction matters for the claim. Use this selectively: an already clear passage does not need a new explanation, and a revision should keep the author's argument and conceptual vocabulary.

For example, a classroom trial gave both practice groups the same mixed review before a delayed test. An original illustration based on Rohrer et al. (2019/2020), DOI 10.1037/edu0000367:

> Both groups received the same mixed review, with one problem of each critical type. This reflected a familiar classroom routine and aligned the interval from final practice to testing. The design thus compared practice arrangements while holding the final review constant.

The last sentence earns its place by naming what the design comparison means. A generic sentence about methodological importance would add less. The passage explains a particular trial; it does not supply a paragraph template or establish that all practice intervals or time spent were equal. In another study, choose the concrete distinction its own evidence requires. Keep that explanation proportionate to the requested passage, and leave editorial checks outside the manuscript.
