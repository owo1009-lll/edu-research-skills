Conventional cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept CLPMs (RI-CLPMs) are often presented as rivals, with the latter treated as uniformly preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We regard them as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept—students’ beliefs about their academic competence—and achievement over time. In this mathematics study, CLPMs compare students’ relative standing, conditional on prior measurements, without separating stable differences from within-student variation; RI-CLPMs estimate lagged relationships between deviations from each student’s usual level. The different comparison levels make both models substantively informative. Between-person associations, common in cross-sectional educational research, do not automatically describe within-person processes or establish causality. Equivalence of between-person and within-person relationships is plausible only under restrictive conditions, including stable parameters over time and consistent relationships across individuals, that are unlikely to hold. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue for examining what these approaches share alongside what each reveals differently.
