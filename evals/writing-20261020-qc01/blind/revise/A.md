Conventional cross-lagged panel models (CLPMs) and random-intercept CLPMs (RI-CLPMs) are often presented as competing approaches, with RI-CLPMs assumed to be inherently preferable (but see Orth et al., 2021). We see these models as complementary perspectives on academic self-concept and achievement. CLPMs compare students’ relative standing, conditional on earlier measurements, without separating stable differences between students from variation within students; RI-CLPMs relate successive departures from each student’s usual level. These distinct comparisons provide useful substantive information, with different strengths and weaknesses. Most educational and psychological research compares people at one time point, yet causal interpretations often exceed what designs or analyses support. Relationships between people need not describe relationships within a person. Equivalence is plausible only under restrictive conditions that are unlikely to hold, including relationships that remain stable over time and similar across individuals. Following Voelkle et al. (2014), we therefore argue that researchers should consider commonalities alongside differences between these perspectives when examining academic self-concept and achievement over time.
