## Research question and educational rationale

The study examines the directional ordering of mathematics self-concept, school mathematics grades and standardized mathematics test scores. Mathematics self-concept concerns students' beliefs about their competence in mathematics. School grades provide visible teacher-assessed feedback in a classroom context; a standardized test supplies another measure of achievement. The authors therefore study both achievement indicators rather than treating them as interchangeable.

The reciprocal-effects model predicts that self-concept and achievement predict one another over time. This contrasts with accounts emphasizing achievement as the precursor of self-concept alone, or self-concept as the precursor of achievement alone. The authors describe a possible cognitive–motivational process: students interpret performance feedback, develop beliefs about their ability, and draw on these beliefs when investing effort or choosing strategies for later tasks. These intervening psychological steps were not measured in the present study.

Much previous work used a conventional cross-lagged panel model (CLPM). A methodological debate questions how the relationships change when stable person differences are separated from variation within persons using a random-intercept cross-lagged panel model (RI-CLPM). The study places the models alongside one another and examines extensions, rather than declaring a model superior on the basis of its name. Claims about the earlier literature are the article authors' review; those cited originals have not been independently read for this exercise.

## Design, measures and comparisons

- The PALMA dataset follows 3,425 students in Bavaria, Germany, from Grades 5 to 9, with annual measurements. It includes three secondary-school tracks. This is a secondary analysis of observational data, not an intervention assigning students to different self-beliefs or teaching practices.
- Mathematics self-concept is a latent construct measured by six questionnaire items. School mathematics grades and standardized mathematics test scores are separate single-indicator achievement constructs.
- Thirty-nine percent of students participated in all five waves; the rest contributed one to four waves. Full-information maximum likelihood retained students with at least one wave. Its missing-data assumptions are not guaranteed by having repeated observations.
- In a CLPM, the lagged associations compare students' relative standing, conditional on preceding measurements; stable between-person differences and within-person variation have not been decomposed. It is not a pure estimate of a separate stable-person process.
- In an RI-CLPM, the lagged associations concern deviations from each student's usual level over the study: a year above that level on self-concept and a subsequent deviation on achievement, or the reverse. The estimated within-person relationship is a common parameter across students, not an individually estimated causal effect for every student.
- Extensions added lag-2 paths and measured covariates: gender and mathematics and German grades from the final year of primary school. The researchers also considered the two achievement measures together in a tripartite model.

## Results available for this passage

Both model families supported positive reciprocal lagged relationships between mathematics self-concept and achievement, using both school grades and standardized test scores. The central pattern remained under the reported lag-2 and covariate extensions. This agreement concerns the pattern under different comparison levels and specifications; it does not make their estimands identical.

Adding lag-2 paths substantially improved the basic CLPM's fit, bringing it closer to the RI-CLPM, while its central cross-lagged pattern was comparatively stable. The article distinguishes changes in fit and stability paths from changes in the relationships central to its substantive question. The RI-CLPM's central paths were less affected by the reported extensions. Measures of explained variance refer to differently decomposed variation in the two families and should not be treated as the same quantity.

The measured covariates had relatively limited influence on the focal paths. The authors explicitly acknowledge that this makes their empirical test of resistance to confounding a weak test. Both approaches remain vulnerable to unmeasured time-varying confounding. The study did not directly assess the proposed feedback–attribution–effort process or test a combined intervention.

## Authors' conceptual position and implications

The authors view the two model perspectives as complementary for understanding relationships between self-concept and achievement. Complementarity here means distinct substantive comparisons considered together, not a statistical interaction or proof that either model identifies causality. They argue that model evaluation should consider the substantive question, parameter interpretation and fit.

Their educational position is that both academic self-beliefs and performance merit attention. They propose a factorial randomized trial crossing a self-concept intervention with an achievement intervention, each present or absent. This could compare the combined programme with each component and with neither. A benefit of the combination is a theoretical prediction, not a result of the present study.

The published discussion cites a meta-analysis by Haney and Durlak (1998) as experimental evidence relevant to self-concept interventions. That original meta-analysis has not been read for this task; no independently verified effect or implementation details are supplied. The current study's contribution can be discussed without treating its review as a new independent verification of that evidence.
