Teachers’ self-reported cooperative learning (CL) implementation was generally higher than observed implementation. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements may contribute. Most participants had not received CL training, and Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise the importance of knowledge in assessing CL. The interviews suggest another explanation: the two teachers with low observed implementation assigned responsibility for cooperation to students and treated social skills as a prerequisite for CL. This perspective may encourage favourable judgements of teaching despite limited student participation, although their individual implementation self-ratings were not reported. The measures also differed: questionnaires covered typical practice and five elements, whereas observations covered four elements in a selected lesson. Further research should examine how knowledge and responsibility for fostering cooperation inform teachers’ and observers’ assessments.
