Teachers generally rated their implementation of cooperative learning (CL) more highly than observers did. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements is one possible explanation; Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise knowledge in assessing CL. Most participants lacked CL training, but training attendance alone does not establish their knowledge. Interviewed teachers with low observed implementation placed responsibility for successful cooperation on students. This perspective may lead teachers to judge their own provision favourably despite poor student participation, although these cases’ individual implementation ratings were not reported. Measurement differences also qualify the comparison: self-reports concern typical practice and five elements, whereas observations assess four elements in a selected lesson. Further research should examine teachers’ and observers’ assessment perspectives, including whether interpretations of responsibility contribute to the discrepancy.
