# Writing rationale

## Before drafting

This is a new, 350–450-word English discussion passage for education researchers and teacher educators. It is an attributed secondary-writing demonstration, not an account of research conducted for this task. The supplied request and source pack are the only substantive evidence. The assigned v2 edu-writing snapshot supplies workflow and writing guidance.

The educational problem is how teachers make cooperation part of instruction when group work is already present. The observations establish sparse provision of the assessed elements in selected lessons. The four contrasting cases give that pattern educational meaning: teachers differ in whether they regard cooperation as something to teach and support or as a competence students should bring to the task. The central judgement is that implementation quality concerns teachers' purposes and responsibility for social learning alongside their use of techniques. The teacher-education implication is to connect planning and responses to difficulties with examination of those purposes.

Evidence supporting this argument: two of 30 observed teachers provided all four assessed elements and 11 provided none; the corresponding extreme cases differed in the value they assigned to social learning, their planning, and their responses to difficulties; one teacher accepted academic goal attainment despite groups failing to cooperate. These cases connect an observation-based distinction to contrasting understandings of the instructional task. They do not establish a statistical mechanism.

Evidence restricting the argument: participation was voluntary, lessons were chosen and requested to include cooperative learning, and the interview comparison contains four selected cases. The observation scale counts one indicator as enough to register an element and omits direct assessment of social skills. It measures instructional provision rather than comprehensive cooperation or learning outcomes. The study neither changes beliefs nor evaluates training programmes.

The passage will move from the implementation problem to the case comparison, then interpret its scope and derive concrete teacher-education priorities. Relevant numerical and measurement qualifications will remain with the claims they define. Design limitations will be grouped. The final sentence will state an educational judgement. Self-report findings will enter only if they advance this argument within the word limit; no attempt will be made to inventory every result.

No new literature, direct interview quotations, corrected source statistics, independent coding, or reanalysis will be introduced. The disputed partial-implementation count, correlation coefficient, scale bound, case matching, and inference about resistance to belief change remain unresolved source matters. The reader-facing note will distinguish these from verified claims used in the passage.

## Revision and final decisions

Draft 01 was saved at 417 body words, checked, and reviewed before draft 02 was written. Draft 02 is the single substantive revision, at 402 body words. It replaces the prevalence implication in “how readily,” places the observation scoring rule beside the observed counts, uses the source-specific communication episode, and brings the overall scope limits together. The final paragraph now centres on the educational reasoning behind the proposed planning activities. Final.md reproduces draft 02 without further substantive changes.

The questionnaire associations and self-report/observation discrepancy were omitted because they require different samples, reference periods, and measurement qualifications. Within 350–450 words, adding them would weaken the sustained argument about observed provision and instructional responsibility. The omission does not convert those findings into absent relationships or resolve their source problems.
