# Discussion

Teachers generally rated their implementation of cooperative learning (CL) more highly than observers did. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements may contribute to this discrepancy; most participants had received no CL training. Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise teachers’ knowledge when assessing CL. The interviews suggest another possible explanation: teachers with low observed implementation placed responsibility for successful cooperation on students, potentially distinguishing their own provision of CL from students’ participation. This perspective could shape self-assessments, although the cases’ individual implementation ratings were not reported. The comparison also involves different reference periods and coverage: self-reports concern typical practice and five elements, whereas observations assess four elements in a selected lesson. Further research should examine how teachers and observers judge implementation, including how their understandings of responsibility affect those judgements.
