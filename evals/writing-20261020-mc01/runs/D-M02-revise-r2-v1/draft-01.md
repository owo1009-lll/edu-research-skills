# Discussion — attributed compression of Adl-Amini, Völlinger, and Eckart (2024), paragraph B068

Teachers generally rated their implementation of cooperative learning (CL) more highly than observers did. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements may partly explain this difference; most participants had received no CL training. Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise the importance of teacher knowledge in assessing CL. Interviews suggest another explanation: teachers with low observed implementation assigned responsibility for cooperation to students, potentially separating their own provision from students’ participation when judging success. This interpretation remains tentative because the cases’ individual implementation ratings were not reported. The questionnaire and observations also covered different periods and elements. Further research should examine how teachers’ knowledge and understandings of responsibility shape their assessments, while accounting for differences between reports of typical practice and observations of selected lessons.
