# Discussion — attributed compression of Adl-Amini, Völlinger, and Eckart (2024), paragraph B068

Teachers generally rated their implementation of cooperative learning (CL) more highly than observers did. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements may partly explain this difference, but most participants’ lack of CL training does not establish a knowledge deficit. Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise the importance of teacher knowledge in assessing CL. In one interview, a teacher with low observed implementation judged the lesson successful despite poor cooperation and attributed communication difficulties to students. This distinction between teaching and student cooperation may inform self-assessments, although its link to individual ratings remains unresolved. The questionnaire also assessed typical practice, whereas observations assessed selected lessons and covered different CL elements. Further research should examine how teachers and observers judge implementation, including the roles of teacher knowledge and responsibility for cooperation.
