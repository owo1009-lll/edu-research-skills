# Writing rationale

## Plan recorded before drafting

Mode: new English discussion passage, 350–450 body words, with a separate attribution heading. This is a secondary-writing demonstration about Adl-Amini, Völlinger, and Eckart's study. Only the supplied source pack supplies substantive evidence. The single-section route requires a first draft, the two applicable checkers, AI self-review, and one substantive revision; full-manuscript confirmation, consistency checks, and Word conversion do not apply.

The educational question is what teachers understand themselves to be responsible for when they organise cooperative learning. The observational evidence establishes sparse provision of the assessed elements even in selected lessons intended to use cooperative learning. Comparing interviews from the observed extremes gives that implementation problem a concrete interpretation: teachers differed over whether cooperation was a competence to teach or a prerequisite to expect. The contribution is this distinction and its connection with planning, responding to difficulties, and judging lesson success. Teacher education can therefore connect technique instruction to examination of responsibility for social learning. The evidence supports an educational priority, not a demonstrated effect of changing beliefs.

Evidence supporting the argument: two of 30 observed teachers provided all four assessed elements, whereas 11 provided none; the interviews contrasted deliberate support and reflection with assumed social competence; one teacher judged academic success sufficient even when most groups had not cooperated. These details connect the meaning of implementation to the criteria teachers use in practice.

Evidence constraining the argument: the four interview cases were selected from observed extremes; lessons were selected by volunteer teachers; observations registered instructional provision, with one indicator sufficient to count an element. The design does not establish that beliefs caused implementation differences or that the proposed teacher-education practices improve outcomes. Self-ratings refer to different practice periods and cover different elements, so they cannot be treated as interchangeable with lesson observations. These boundaries will remain beside the relevant interpretation or together in the closing discussion.

Proposed progression: introduce the implementation problem and its observed scope; use the case contrast to explain what the study adds; distinguish what self-ratings and observation can support; derive specific teacher-education work, with the limits needed to assess that recommendation.

No additional literature will be supplied. No direct participant quotations will be invented from paraphrases. No claim will rely on the inconsistent partial-implementation total, unresolved correlation decimal, suitability-scale bound, exact matching claim, or resistance-to-change inference. The separate delivery note will record unresolved checks without crowding the passage.

Source locations available only through the pack: concepts and observation coverage; design and correspondence of sources; findings available for writing; published paragraph's context; authorial position, implications and scope. The original article, its raw materials, and cited originals have not been independently accessed in this run.

## Revision decisions and final correspondence

The saved first draft contained a separate paragraph about higher self-ratings. After checking and AI self-review, the revision removed that branch to concentrate the short discussion on the implementation question. This is a choice about the new passage's focus, not a correction or rejection of the published authors' argument. The revised opening identifies the educational distinction; the case paragraph explains its practical meaning; a scope paragraph states what the design and observation scale establish; the closing paragraph connects the evidence to teacher education.
