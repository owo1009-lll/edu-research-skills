Teachers rated their cooperative learning (CL) implementation more highly than observers did. Limited knowledge of CL’s basic elements may contribute to this discrepancy: most participants had received no CL training, and Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) emphasise knowledge when assessing CL. The interviews suggest another explanation. Teachers with low observed implementation placed responsibility for cooperation on students, potentially separating their own teaching from students’ participation when judging success. This interpretation could help explain favourable self-ratings, although the cases’ individual implementation ratings were not reported. Differences between measures also matter: questionnaires concerned typical practice and five elements, whereas observations covered four elements in one selected lesson. Further research should examine how teachers and observers judge implementation, particularly how knowledge and responsibility for fostering cooperation enter those judgements.
