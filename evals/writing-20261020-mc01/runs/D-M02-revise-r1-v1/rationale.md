# Revision rationale

## Argument map prepared before the first draft

The original B068 paragraph reports a discrepancy between teachers’ self-reported and observed implementation quality. It proposes limited knowledge of cooperative learning (CL) elements as one explanation, connects that possibility to limited training, and retains Hennessey and Dionigi (2013) as support for knowledge’s relevance to assessment. It then uses interview accounts of responsibility to propose that teachers could regard their own implementation as successful while attributing failed cooperation to students. It closes by calling for research into teachers’ and observers’ perspectives. This assessment problem, the knowledge explanation, the responsibility explanation, the existing citation, and the purposeful closing remain the paragraph’s core.

The supplied context supports generally higher self-ratings and the interview distinction between teachers’ instructional responsibility and students’ participation. It does not display individual implementation self-ratings for the contrasting interview cases; self-efficacy in Table 3 is a different construct. Thus the original’s “As a result, they rated…” conclusion needs a specific repair. Lack of CL training is also not a direct knowledge assessment. Finally, the questionnaire and observations have different reference periods and coverage, which matters for interpreting the discrepancy.

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Cooperative learning (CL) | Students working in small groups to help one another learn; the full framework has five basic elements. |
| Self-reported implementation | Teachers’ assessments of typical practice. Distinct from self-efficacy. |
| Observed implementation | Instructional indicators covering four elements in a selected lesson. Distinct from a direct measure of students’ engagement or learning. |
| Responsibility for cooperation | Whether teachers view cooperation as something to foster or assume students should supply it. |
