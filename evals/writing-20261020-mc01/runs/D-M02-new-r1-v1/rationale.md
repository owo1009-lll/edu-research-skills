# Writing rationale

## Pre-draft argument

This is a new, 350–450-word English discussion passage, not a revision of the published paragraph. It is a secondary-writing demonstration based exclusively on the supplied source pack. The passage will use an attribution heading outside the word count and connected paragraphs without internal headings. The educational question is what classroom implementation requires when teachers ask pupils to work cooperatively. The observational evidence identifies sparse provision of the assessed elements in selected lessons. The comparison of four interviews, selected using observation scores, supplies a substantive distinction: teachers can regard cooperation as a competence they help develop or as a prerequisite students should already possess. The central contribution is the concrete connection between this distinction and reported planning, responses to difficulties, and criteria for judging success. This is a case-grounded interpretation, not an identified causal effect of beliefs.

The argument will proceed from the implementation problem to the contrasting cases, explain the limits of that comparison, and derive teacher-education actions concerning planning and reflection. These actions elaborate the authors' position that beliefs and responsibility should be addressed alongside techniques. Their effectiveness has not been tested here.

## Evidence supporting the argument

- Only two of 30 observed teachers provided all four assessed elements; 11 provided none. These endpoint counts are consistent in the source pack.
- In the two cases with all four assessed elements, teachers described cooperation as a competence to develop, attended to exchange and reflection, and reported adjusting groups or roles.
- The contrasting cases treated social skills as a prerequisite. One judged a lesson successful on academic grounds although most groups had not cooperated, illustrating how criteria for success can omit the cooperative process.
- The authors explicitly recommend teacher education addressing beliefs and responsibility for social learning alongside CL techniques.

## Evidence constraining the argument

- Volunteer recruitment and teacher-selected lessons do not support prevalence estimates for German teachers generally.
- Four extreme cases give a detailed contrast, not a representative distribution or a causal test. The study does not follow changes in beliefs or manipulate them.
- The observation scale records instructional provision for four elements, with at least one indicator sufficient per element. It does not directly measure social skills or establish student learning gains.
- The reported perspectives must remain distinct from observed instructional provision. No independent interview coding or raw-data analysis is available for this task.
