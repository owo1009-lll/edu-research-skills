# Rationale and writing record

## Plan recorded before drafting

This is a new English discussion passage for education researchers and teacher educators. It is a secondary-writing demonstration based solely on the supplied source pack, not a report of original research or a reanalysis. The body will contain 350–450 words, with one separate attribution heading and no internal subheadings. The requested output directory and Markdown format take precedence over the skill's default directory and Word delivery.

The educational question is what teachers must attend to when implementing cooperative learning. The central argument will be that the study gives instructional content to teachers' responsibility for cooperation: the contrasting cases distinguish teaching cooperation from assuming that students already know how to cooperate. The observations establish an implementation problem in selected lessons; the case comparison helps interpret what that problem means for planning and responding to difficulties. This is a conceptual and practical contribution, not a demonstrated causal effect of beliefs.

Evidence supporting the argument includes the two lessons containing all four assessed elements, the 11 containing none, the contrasting interview accounts of developing social competence, and one teacher's judgment of academic success despite unsuccessful cooperation. Evidence restricting the argument includes volunteer recruitment, selected lessons, four extreme cases, observation of instructional provision rather than student learning, and a scoring rule satisfied by one indicator per element. Both sets will shape the body. The comparison concerns the authors' analysis of interviews collected immediately after lessons and subsequently selected using observation scores.

The passage will move from the implementation problem to the meaning gained by comparing cases, then to the scope of that inference and concrete teacher-education implications. Recommendations will connect preparation in roles, groups, and reflection with examination of teachers' responsibility when interaction fails. Their value is an argument for programme design, not a tested programme effect.

The survey associations and the self-report/observation discrepancy will be omitted to preserve this argument within the word limit. They concern different samples, reference periods, and indicators and cannot establish individual correspondence with the four cases. No additional literature, corrected statistics, quotations, or exact case matching will be introduced.

## Revision and delivery record

The complete 405-word first draft was saved before checker execution and AI self-review. One complete revision then produced `revised-draft.md`, a 381-word body. The revised passage describes the counts as properties of observed lessons, explicitly identifies observation-guided interview selection, and makes the interpretation about responsibility concrete in planning, monitoring, and revision decisions. It removes the unsupported rhetorical weight of “especially consequential.” It retains the authors' substantive position that teacher education should address responsibility for social learning alongside techniques.

Both required checkers were run again on the revision. Their actual output is saved separately. The remaining low-citation-density prompt was retained because this is a single-source exercise. The absence of self-mentions preserves attribution. The first draft, review, and revised draft remain separate files; `final.md` reproduces the checked revision. `notes.md` is the short delivery note. No consistency checker, Word conversion, statistical reanalysis, browsing, or external source verification was performed.
