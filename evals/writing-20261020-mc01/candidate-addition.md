## Show the meaning gained by connecting findings

In mixed-methods writing, explain what one source adds to the reader's understanding of another. The useful connection may concern a contrast, a decision, the meaning of an activity or the conditions of participation. Choose the connection supported by this study; an already clear passage does not need an extra integration paragraph.

An original illustration based on Lin-Aung et al. (2022), DOI 10.5116/ijme.6399.dee1:

> At this medical school, survey respondents more often reported gains in self-learning than in communication. Focus-group accounts described practice in explaining ideas alongside anxiety about speaking incorrectly. One participant described an interaction as skill-building while disliking it. Read together, these findings distinguish perceived learning value from comfort in participation.

The final sentence names the recognition gained from the connection. It does more work than saying that two methods provide a comprehensive picture. The example relates group-level survey descriptions and participants' accounts, not matched answers from the same individuals or independently measured skill gains. Another design may connect findings through case selection, instrument development or a disagreement that remains unresolved. Preserve that design's actual relationship and the author's substantive position when revising; do not import this example's conceptual contrast or turn it into a fixed sequence of sentences.
