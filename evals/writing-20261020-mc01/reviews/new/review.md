# Independent anonymous review: new English discussion

This is a single-pass model review of A–D against the supplied request and source pack. Only the seven permitted input files were read. No browsing, attribution key, other review, manuscript edit, or independent article verification was used. Attribution headings and separate editorial/source-check notes were intentionally withheld and are not treated as missing deliverables. No human-review or cross-model claim is made.

Paragraph references refer to the four paragraphs in each body. Word counts use whitespace tokens solely to check the 350–450-word requirement: A, 396; B, 392; C, 402; D, 381. All comply. These counts are not a quality measure.

## Comparative judgment

All four belong in the same substantive quality tier. Each offers a defensible discussion, accurately joins observed provision with teachers' accounts of responsibility, and derives appropriately bounded teacher-education implications. No robust overall winner is warranted.

B and D have a slight editorial advantage for a standalone discussion. B is clearest about analytic selection and the distinct contribution of observations and interviews. D is especially compact and clear about assessed arrangements, interpretation and application. These are small presentational advantages, not a demonstrated difference in factual soundness or argument adequacy. A states the competence-versus-prerequisite problem especially well. C provides the most specific evidence-to-application link by retaining the teacher's omission of a communication problem from planning.

The overall judgment is therefore **A = B = C = D in substantive quality**, with **B = D as a low-confidence presentational preference**. Confidence is high that all four meet the task and contain no material error relative to the pack; confidence is low that small stylistic differences warrant an overall ordering. A reader's preference for explicit methodological explanation or concrete case detail could reasonably change the marginal preference.

## Shared findings

- All four meet the requested 350–450-word range, use connected prose without internal subheadings, and attribute the study to its authors.
- All use the consistent endpoint counts and distinguish four assessed instructional elements from comprehensive evidence of cooperation or learning.
- All derive the teacher-responsibility argument from the case contrast and distinguish proposed teacher-education applications from demonstrated intervention effects.
- The observation and interview evidence jointly support the argument: observed provision locates and differentiates implementation, while selected interviews illuminate purposes, responsibility and success criteria. None establishes that changing beliefs causes improved implementation.
- Focusing on observation and interviews is a legitimate response to the new-writing task. Omission of questionnaire results, the published paragraph's self-report discrepancy and unrelated unresolved source anomalies is not a defect.
- All could optionally identify the lower-scoring comparison pair as the two teachers with none of the assessed elements. Their references to contrasting cases and extreme-case selection already make the comparison intelligible; this is a precision edit, not a factual correction.

Evidence: P1: “The interview comparison then brings a specific educational question into focus”; P2: “the criterion for success allowed unaddressed difficulties in cooperation to coexist with a positive judgement”.

Evidence: P2: “the authors' argument that responsibility for social learning belongs within implementation”; P4: “structure interdependence and individual accountability”.

**Strong.** The implications identify concrete work for teacher educators: examining planning, role assignments, criteria for success and group reflection. The recommendations are assertive enough to be useful and do not claim that beliefs have experimentally established effects.

Evidence: P2: “In this case, the criterion for success allowed unaddressed difficulties in cooperation to coexist with a positive judgement of the lesson”; P4: “teachers should examine what they plan to teach”.

If more explicit case selection is desired, identify the contrasting pair as teachers whose observed lessons contained none of the four assessed elements.
