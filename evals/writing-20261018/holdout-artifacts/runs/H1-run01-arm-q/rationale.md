# Writing rationale and evidence record

## Scope and pre-draft argument

New English Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion, 600–750 words excluding references, for a secondary writing exercise. The source study remains Dunster et al. (2018); the writer does not claim to have conducted it. Only H1-materials.md supplies study evidence. Its full-text and DOI links are identifiers supplied by the packet, not pages independently accessed here. The requested Markdown files are the delivery format; no Word conversion is needed. This is new writing, so a separate change_log.md is not required.

**Educational problem:** An early school morning may constrain adolescents' opportunity to sleep. A policy judgment therefore needs to distinguish actual additional sleep from simply shifting the timetable, and to examine accompanying educational outcomes.

**Evidence answer:** Two independent tenth-grade cohorts were observed before and seven months after Seattle's timetable change. Objective recordings showed 34 minutes longer median school-night sleep, alongside later waking. Biology grades were higher; first-period attendance improved significantly at Franklin but not Roosevelt. These outcomes come from different measurement systems and coverage.

**Core contribution:** Objective field observations months after a district-wide change connect school scheduling to adolescents' sleep opportunity. The educational outcomes make the policy relevant to learning and access to lessons, while requiring separate judgments about measurement and causal attribution.

**Scope:** Observed annual-cohort differences, not identified intervention effects, individual sleep-mediated achievement gains, or demonstrated reductions in socioeconomic gaps. The conclusion should retain this distinction without becoming a list of disclaimers.

## Supporting and qualifying evidence

Supporting evidence: two weeks of actigraphy checked against diaries, event markers and visual inspection; later waking and longer school-night sleep; comparable teachers, grade level and season; observation seven months after implementation; higher biology marks and improved first-period attendance at Franklin.

Qualifying evidence: independent cohorts and no concurrent comparison schools; onset and nonschool-night differences were nonsignificant, not proven equivalent; unstandardized grades and teacher awareness; Roosevelt attendance changes were nonsignificant; attendance records do not cover the same population as actigraphy; no individual causal mediation or socioeconomic moderation analysis. The source has unresolved reporting discrepancies, so no unprovided p values, intervals, corrected tests or precision claims will be supplied.
