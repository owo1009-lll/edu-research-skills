3. **Remove an unnecessary biological-clock disclaimer.** The first sleep paragraph closes by saying that the measures did not establish a biological-clock shift, although it has not asserted such a shift. Remove that trailing disclaimer and end with the educational interpretation of sleep opportunity. Retain the correct scope of the actual findings; no new physiological mechanism is introduced.

4. **Strengthen the grade paragraph's reasoning.** Keep 77.5%, 82.0%, and the 4.5-percentage-point difference, together with teacher awareness and unstandardized marking. Replace the redundant negative comparison with standardized tests and the generic sentence about performance being relevant with a concrete consequence: evaluate achievement using measures comparable across implementation years. The lack of an identified sleep-to-grade pathway is essential and stays beside the plausible sleep explanation.

5. **Make attendance interpretable as access to lessons.** Retain first-period and school-wide coverage, both schools' findings, and the distinction from actigraphy participants. Replace the lengthy significance tutorial with a precise statement that whether benefits differed by school or student socioeconomic circumstances remains unresolved. State the educational implication as evaluating missed classroom time at individual schools. No formal interaction or socioeconomic moderation estimate is supplied, so a claim of greater benefit for disadvantaged students is unavailable.

6. **Consolidate design limitations and calibrate follow-up.** Keep comparable teachers, grade level and season alongside independent cohorts and the lack of concurrent comparison schools. A proposal to follow students and include unchanged-schedule schools addresses cohort change and general time trends; it does not automatically identify causality. Do not imply that the proposed design has already been implemented.

7. **Carry one answer into the conclusion.** Preserve the association with school-night sleep and name the educational contribution: school scheduling is a practical part of students' sleep opportunity. Educational outcomes should be assessed separately because they do not share the sleep measure's coverage or interpretation. Avoid promising universal achievement or equity improvements.
