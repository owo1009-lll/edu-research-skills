## Author's argument reconstructed before drafting

The original Discussion asks how universities can reach students with inadequate food access and address the conditions under which it occurs. Its organizing links are: (1) sociodemographic and academic associations identify groups needing outreach; (2) disability and dietary accounts show that available food is not always usable food; (3) housing, mental health and finances are interconnected with food security; (4) existing services offer reported benefits, but a low prevalence relative to other studies is not an established service effect; and (5) coordinated university action should address immediate provision, underlying costs and continuing access to support. The conclusion places these recommendations within students' ability to pursue their degrees. The original contains no formal theoretical framework to replace.

The revision retains that practical position. Its clearest contribution is to connect the distribution of insecurity with concrete barriers to obtaining and preparing suitable food, giving universities reasons to coordinate food, housing, financial and mental-health support. It does not claim a new general mechanism or a tested intervention effect. The requested compression permits the original recurring topic/recommendation pairs to become evidence-to-action paragraphs.

## Evidence supporting and qualifying the central judgment

Supporting: low/very low food security in both undergraduate and graduate respondent groups; associations with housing, finances, disability and academic performance; distinct accounts of shopping/cooking time, dietary restrictions and couch-surfing; welcomed food support; and a practical account of unsuitable packages. Together these support coordinated and adaptable support as the authors' professional judgment.

Qualifying: voluntary response and sample imbalance; one pandemic-era university; associations without established direction or a demonstrated fully adjusted causal model; wide uncertainty for temporary/unstable housing; unequal comparability with earlier studies; sparse written accounts; ongoing academic stress despite meal relief; and help from a friend as well as the service. These are carried into the relevant Discussion paragraphs or its consolidated limits paragraph. The short Conclusion states an institutional priority rather than a validated treatment effect.

## Author/source queries kept outside the manuscript

2. Table 4 denominators differ from the 2,116 complete responses. Do not pair subgroup prevalence with the complete-response denominator or imply that it is the sum of the displayed groups.
3. The packet does not establish full mutual adjustment for the reported odds ratios. Avoid independent-predictor and causal interpretations.
4. Other studies are available only through Hall et al.'s review. Do not independently cite their mechanisms or reproduce the original numbered references without their list.
5. Raw data, supplementary material, most figure images and peer review were not audited here. All checks are against the supplied packet, not a fresh source audit.
6. The short form omits some original details for relevance and length, not because an unavailable source is proven wrong. Specific omissions are recorded in change_log.md.
