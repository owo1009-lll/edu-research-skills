# Writing rationale and evidence record

## Scope and planned argument

New English Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion, 600–750 words excluding references. This is a secondary writing exercise about Dunster et al. (2018), not a new study or a submission in the writer's name. The request authorizes direct writing; no outline approval is needed. Only the supplied H1 evidence packet informs factual claims.

Question → Does a later school start correspond to longer school-night sleep, and what educational changes accompany it?
Evidence → Independent tenth-grade cohorts in two Seattle schools were compared before and seven months after a district schedule change. Objective wrist recordings show a 34-minute longer median school-night sleep duration. Biology marks were higher; school-wide first-period attendance changes differed descriptively across schools.
Core judgment → School scheduling deserves consideration as a condition supporting adolescents' opportunity to sleep. The distinctive contribution is observation of realized sleep months after a district change, rather than an assumption that extra morning time becomes sleep.
Scope → This before-and-after comparison is strongest as field evidence of a sleep opportunity. Cohort and year effects remain unresolved; educational outcomes do not establish a sleep-mediated achievement effect or socioeconomic gap reduction.

## Evidence supporting and qualifying the central judgment

Supporting evidence:
- Median school-night sleep: 6 h 50 min versus 7 h 24 min; waking 44 minutes later. Sleep onset tended later but did not meet the adjusted significance threshold (Results B010–B011, B015; Discussion B019).
- Two weeks of actigraphy checked using diaries, event markers and visual inspection; observations seven months after implementation (Results B009; Participants B028; Methods).
- No significant differences in nonschool-night timing or duration; lower sleepiness and social jet lag provide compatible but distinct observations (B012, B015).
- Classes, teachers, grade level and season were comparable (B009; B028).

Qualifying evidence:
- Independent annual cohorts and no concurrent unchanged-schedule group leave cohort differences and year-specific influences unresolved (B009; B028).
- Biology marks were absolute, unstandardized teacher grades; teachers knew the schedule change. No individual causal mediation analysis (Figure 3A; B025; B045).
- First-period school-wide attendance is separate from the actigraphy sample. A significant result in Franklin and a nonsignificant result in Roosevelt do not establish a between-school difference or socioeconomic moderation (Figure 3C–D; B017; B046).
- Unresolved reporting issues are recorded below; no raw-data reanalysis or confidence intervals are available.
