# Writing rationale and evidence record

## Scope and main line established before drafting

This is new English writing for a bounded secondary exercise, not a revision of an author manuscript or a report of research conducted by the writer. Deliver Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion totalling 600-750 words, excluding references. The sole substantive source is the supplied H1 evidence packet about Dunster et al. (2018). No full text, supplementary file, original cited study or raw data is independently read in this run. No web access is used. The manuscript identifies its secondary status and uses third-person attribution.

**Educational question:** When a school system moves its morning start later, is the additional time reflected in students' sleep, and what educational changes accompany it?

**What the evidence answers:** In two Seattle public high schools, independent tenth-grade cohorts assessed before and seven months after the schedule change differed in objectively recorded school-night sleep. Biology grades were higher in the later cohort; school-wide first-period attendance changes were reported at one school. These outcomes differ in measurement and coverage.

**Core contribution:** The study places objectively observed sleep alongside educational outcomes under an implemented district policy. It makes school scheduling relevant to the conditions in which students attend and learn, while distinguishing an observed sleep opportunity from demonstrated academic or equity effects.

**Scope:** Between-year association after a real policy change; no concurrent unchanged-schedule comparison and no individual mediation test. Educational claims remain specific to teacher-assigned biology grades and school-wide first-period attendance.

## Supporting evidence

- Two-week wrist actigraphy, checked against diaries, event markers and visual inspection, provides an objective account beyond sleep survey reports (packet: Methods; Introduction B005-B007).
- School-night median sleep was 6 h 50 min versus 7 h 24 min; median waking was 44 minutes later. Observations occurred seven months after implementation, with comparable season, teachers, classes and grade level (Results B009-B011; Participants B028).
- School-night observations and the lack of significant nonschool-night differences fit a school-schedule interpretation; this is not an equivalence test (Results B015; Discussion B019).
- Median biology grades were 77.5% versus 82.0%; first-period attendance reductions were reported at Franklin (Figure 3; B017).

## Evidence limiting the main interpretation

- Independent annual cohorts and no concurrent control leave cohort differences and other changes over time unresolved.
- Sleep onset tended later without meeting the adjusted significance threshold. Nonsignificance does not establish unchanged onset or unchanged nonschool-night sleep.
- Grades were absolute, unstandardized teacher marks; teachers knew the policy changed. No individual sleep-to-grade causal mediation analysis is supplied.
- Attendance coverage is school-wide and first-period only; it is distinct from the wrist-recording sample. Significant change at Franklin and nonsignificant change at Roosevelt is not a formal between-school contrast. Socioeconomic moderation and achievement-gap reduction were not established.
- Inferential reporting issues remain unresolved in the publication. Descriptive values are checked in the packet, but no reanalysis or confidence intervals are available. Avoid supplying missing inferential precision or making significance the argument's foundation.
