## Substantive revisions to make

1. **Clarify the measured category.** The opening correctly reports 15.9% and 16.5% for undergraduate and graduate respondents. However, the later phrase "low food security" could be read as excluding the very low category. Define their combination as food insecurity at first mention and use that label in the odds-ratio paragraph. Preserve broader food access as a separate concept. This resolves an expression ambiguity rather than changing estimates.
2. **Strengthen the practical reasoning for structural support.** Housing odds are correctly distinguished from risk, and the wide interval 2.58–94.58 is retained. Borrowing and cutting other essential spending justify attention to budgets, but the paragraph can explain that these recommendations address pressures beyond immediate food provision. Retain housing, compensation, dining subsidies and employment without presenting them as tested interventions. Describe the 2022 resolution specifically as a strategic-goal/funding-plan commitment.
3. **Clarify chronology around experienced help.** In first.md the pilot and formal-launch dates follow the accounts. Move the chronology to the start of that paragraph: the survey was in April 2021, during a pilot begun in 2020, before the 2022 launch. This prevents readers from attaching survey experiences to the later formal program. Keep emergency grants and local-resource information as existing support.
4. **Use the variation in accounts more directly.** Keep the friend who cooked alongside university help; keep continuing academic stress in the separate speaker; retain the third speaker's food-package concern. Add a conditional recommendation to consider voucher options where fixed packages do not meet students' dietary and preparation needs. This is grounded in the participant suggestion and original dietary recommendations, not a demonstrated voucher effect.
5. **Refine limitations and future research.** The first draft concentrates overall design limitations appropriately. Explain the cross-sectional limitation through the specific unresolved ordering between food insecurity and academic difficulty. Separate repeated observations of transitions from assessment of academic-calendar recall periods; longitudinal design alone does not resolve measurement. State clearly that the proposed actions have not been evaluated for food or educational outcomes.
6. **Keep the supported authorial position.** "Universities should" remains appropriate for reviewing provision, coordinating support and sustained outreach. Do not weaken all recommendations because the survey is cross-sectional. The restriction concerns demonstrated intervention effects, not whether the authors can make reasoned institutional judgments.
