## Task and author argument reconstructed before drafting

This is an attributed secondary adaptation of Hall et al. (2024), not a manuscript by the study authors or a proposed submission. Revise only the supplied Discussion, limitations and Conclusion, using the accompanying evidence packet. Deliver one English Discussion and a short Conclusion, 650–800 words excluding references. The user expressly permits merging and reorganizing paragraphs and requests Markdown; no Word conversion or main-line approval is required.

The original argument asks which university students experience inadequate food access and what institutional responses follow. It connects socio-demographic and economic associations to outreach; dietary barriers to suitable campus food; housing and financial insecurity to affordability; mental distress to coordinated referrals; and reported assistance experiences to accessible support. Its conclusion advocates coordinated provision that supports students pursuing degrees. The source's educational concern is the time, stress and material demands of securing food while studying. No named explanatory theory is advanced, and no new framework is imposed here.

The revision will retain that practical position. Its organizing claim is that identifying students who report food insecurity should lead to support they can actually obtain, prepare and use. Survey associations describe differences in reported food security; written survey statements explain concrete barriers and variation in assistance experiences. Their integration refines service design without estimating program effects or reconstructing a single student trajectory.

## Evidence supporting and constraining the central judgment

Supporting evidence: low/very low food security among both undergraduate and graduate respondents; housing, disability and GPA associations; accounts of shopping/cooking burdens and competing study demands; distinct reports of help and unsuitable food packages; original recommendations connecting food, housing, financial and mental health services.

Constraining evidence to keep visible: low voluntary response at one institution; overrepresentation of graduates and first-generation students; modified self-report measurement with a 12-month recall period; cross-sectional associations; wide interval for temporary/unstable housing; assistance accounts with other concurrent help and persistent academic stress; lower prevalence across dissimilar studies cannot identify an institutional program effect. The written statements supply experiences, not estimates of their prevalence.
