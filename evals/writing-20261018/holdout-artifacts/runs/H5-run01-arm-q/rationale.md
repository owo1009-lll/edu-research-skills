## Argument planned before drafting

Homework policy needs to distinguish introducing homework from increasing its duration. Guo et al. report a positive homework-versus-none pooled estimate but only two heterogeneous comparisons of amounts. The contribution is to clarify which policy question the evidence answers: the case for purposeful supplementary homework is stronger than the case for a daily duration rule. Outcomes, content allocation, and follow-up complicate the translation from time spent to learning. Recommendations should preserve supplementary homework as an option while directing evaluation toward intended skills and retained learning.

## Evidence supporting and restricting the argument

Supporting: homework-versus-none g = 0.45 [0.24, 0.66]; positive arithmetic computation and problem-solving estimates; Guo et al.'s recommendation for primary-school supplementation. Restricting: arithmetic concepts interval includes negative and positive estimates; no formal subdomain contrast; only two amount comparisons with different operations and outcomes; amount and subject allocation intertwined in the contemporary comparison; later writing differences unclear; no established duration-response function. No included study was low risk overall, studies are largely historical paper-and-pencil assignments, and search/retrieval coverage restricts current reach.
