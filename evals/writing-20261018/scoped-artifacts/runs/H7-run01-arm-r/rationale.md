## Author argument reconstructed before drafting

The original asks how students come to integrate research knowledge, skills and attitudes within a compulsory, five-semester medical research module. Its argument moves from a proposed sequence of eight learning activities, through higher grades in later project courses, to the educational judgment that students need to conduct supervised research. Situated learning and participation in a community of practice explain why authentic projects might make earlier instruction meaningful. The authors identify delayed appreciation as the distinctive insight, compare it with their reading of evaluation and transfer models, and allow the apparent sequence to be iterative. They recommend experiential research modules and propose longer-term evaluation.

The supported contribution is an account of when previously abstract research instruction becomes meaningful to students, organized through the 8A-Model and interpreted alongside course-stage grade patterns. Preserve this account, its named framework and the recommendation for supervised research. Repair the causal chain from appreciation to performance and onward to patient benefit. Do not turn the text into a methodological critique or a replacement theory.

## Supporting and limiting evidence

Supporting: the model's four paired themes; students' accounts of early confusion and later relevance, critical appraisal, communication and perceived transfer; higher grades in project courses; similar reported grade pattern within the oldest cohort; authentic projects, supervision and stable instructors/rubrics; four oldest-cohort participants' member checking.

Limiting: focus groups reconstruct stages across cohorts rather than track individual change; course grades assess different tasks and the available cohort composition changes; all-course GPA contains research-course GPA; qualitative and quantitative evidence are integrated at curriculum stage, not matched by person; the module has no experimental or comparison-program design; member checking is not prospective validation; no patient, cost or career outcomes were measured. These constraints affect distinct inferences and will be placed accordingly.
