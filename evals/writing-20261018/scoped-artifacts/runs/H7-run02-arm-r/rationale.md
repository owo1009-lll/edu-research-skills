# Revision rationale

## Scope and author argument, recorded before drafting

This is a 650–800-word attributed English secondary revision of the supplied Discussion and Conclusion of Otaki et al. (2023). It is not an author-approved manuscript. Only the supplied packet and excerpt supply empirical or literature evidence. The requested deliverable is Markdown; no Word export is needed. The user explicitly authorizes reorganization, merging and direct execution, so no outline confirmation is required.

The original argument runs from the 8A-Model, through an apparent transition in grades at project application, to an experiential-learning recommendation. Delayed appreciation qualifies the authors' reading of learning-transfer models. Their situated-learning account links authentic participation, mentors, reflection and knowledge use. They treat the framework as iterative despite its sequential diagram and propose wider and longitudinal study. The revision retains that argument, the named paired concepts, and the force of the recommendation for supervised undergraduate research. It repairs unsupported links from appreciation to grades and from research participation to patient benefit.

The contribution is a curriculum-level interpretation of when students come to recognize the value of research: project application gives previously disconnected methods a purpose. Grades locate a corresponding curricular transition; narratives describe its educational meaning. Neither source alone, nor their convergence, establishes an individual causal pathway.

## Evidence supporting and constraining the core interpretation

Supporting: reports of early confusion and later relevance (6.1, 7.2, 8.2); mentor communication (9.2), research questions (11.2), critical appraisal (3.1, 7.2), accomplishment and perceived transfer (14.3, 12.3); higher grades in later project courses; a similar reported grade pattern in the oldest cohort; the authors' situated-learning rationale and iterative framework.

Constraining: course assessments change; cohort availability changes; interview accounts and grade changes are not linked at individual level; one focus group per cohort reconstructs progression; one institution and no comparison intervention; all-course GPA includes research courses; unreported precision and incomplete support for gender claims. The framework's Affect component represents intended contribution, not observed medical or public-health effects.
