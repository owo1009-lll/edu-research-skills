# Writing rationale and evidence ledger

## Main line established before drafting

**Mode and scope:** New English Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion for an explicitly identified secondary writing exercise. Target: 600?750 words excluding references. These are selected sections, not a complete manuscript; no Methods or Results sections, Word conversion or external literature search are required. User authorization to execute directly removes any need for a main-line approval step.

**Educational question:** Can a deliberately structured AI tutor support initial learning of introductory physics more effectively than an established active-learning class, and what teaching decision follows from that comparison?

**What the evidence answers:** A randomized crossover in one Harvard course compared two instructional packages across two lessons. Immediate quiz results favored the AI package against experienced teachers using the same objectives and worksheet content. The package combined expert content, sequenced interaction, feedback, a recorded introduction, home study and individual pacing.

**Core contribution:** Evidence that designed AI tutoring can improve immediate initial learning relative to a substantial active comparator. The educational distinction is between an instructional design implemented with AI and generic access to a chatbot. The study does not identify the contribution of each component or establish the effectiveness of a whole-course blended arrangement.

**Scope:** Introductory physics for life-science students, two topics, one university, immediate topic learning. Preserve that scope in the answer and conclusion; explain mechanism uncertainty where mechanisms are discussed; concentrate the remaining design boundaries in one discussion paragraph.
