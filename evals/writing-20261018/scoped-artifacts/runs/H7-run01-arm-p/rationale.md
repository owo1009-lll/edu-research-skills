## Original argument reconstructed

The original Discussion asks how students come to understand and use research through an integrated undergraduate medical research module. It introduces the 8A-Model as an account of this development, connects later project work and higher grades at the Apply stage, and explains the delayed recognition of research's value through situated learning and a community of practice. It compares that timing with the authors' reading of Kirkpatrick and learning-transfer models. The authors recommend supervised experiential research within undergraduate curricula, describe the model as iterative, and propose longitudinal and cross-institutional work. The Conclusion repeats the importance of application and advocates an experiential component.

Preserve that educational argument and the four paired concepts. Repair the specific passages that convert curriculum-level convergence into individual causal development, appreciation into a mediator, grade differences into demonstrated competence gains, or aspirations into patient benefit. The supported recommendation remains a clear 'should'; uncertainty about causal effects does not erase the professional judgment.

## Evidence supporting the central judgment

- The five-semester module combines research-methods teaching with students' supervised projects (setting, B003–B041).
- Accounts distinguish early confusion from later relevance during application; mentor communication, critical appraisal and reported transfer make the learning interpretation concrete (findings, B042–B108; participants 6.1, 7.2, 8.2, 9.2, 3.1, 12.3, 14.3).
- Research-course GPA is higher in later project courses; the paper reports an early/later contrast and a similar pattern in the oldest cohort (Figure 2; findings, B042–B108).
- The authors explicitly describe the 8A-Model as potentially iterative and overlapping, despite the sequence in its diagram (Figures 1–3).

## Evidence that narrows the judgment

- Cohort focus groups reconstruct development; they do not repeatedly interview the same students. The joint display does not pair each interviewee's narrative with personal grade change.
- Tasks and cohort composition differ by course. All-course GPA includes research courses. Grades are not an invariant competence measure; there is no comparison intervention.
- Gender claims lack sufficient supplied inferential support. Admission standardization is a conjecture.
- Member checking by four oldest-cohort participants is not prospective independent validation. Patient benefit, costs and later careers were not measured.
- Course-record availability differs between Methods and Results/Table 2. Keep this unresolved issue out of revised quantitative claims and record it below.
