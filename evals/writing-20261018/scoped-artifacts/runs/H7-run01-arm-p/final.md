## Discussion

Across cohort accounts and course records, this study locates students' growing appreciation of research in the transition from learning methods to using them in supervised projects. The 8A-Model organizes this interpretation into four paired themes: Attend–Acquire, Accumulate–Assimilate, Apply–Appreciate, and Articulate–Affect. In this undergraduate medical research module, the framework connects acquiring knowledge with recognizing its relevance through participation. Although depicted sequentially, these processes can overlap and recur as students revisit concepts in new tasks. Its contribution is an account of why research learning may become meaningful after initial instruction: application gives previously disconnected knowledge a recognizable purpose.

Students' accounts make that interpretation concrete. One participant described early biostatistics and software work as confusing and disconnected (6.1), whereas others recognized relevance during project work (7.2 and 8.2). A participant described examining reported numbers instead of simply accepting authors' interpretations (7.2). Communication with supervisors was itself a learning experience (9.2), and another participant reported accomplishment and using presentation skills during internships (14.3). These accounts fit the authors' situated-learning interpretation of authentic activity within a community of practice, supported by experienced researchers [26,61]. Articulate–Affect extends the framework toward communicating findings and contributing beyond the course; effects on medicine and public health remain an ambition, not measured outcomes.

The grade pattern converges with these accounts at the transition to project work. Mean research-course grade point averages (GPAs) were 3.01, 2.94 and 2.95 in the first three semesters, followed by 3.35 and 3.58 in the fourth and fifth, on a 0–4 scale. The paper reports a contrast between these early and later course clusters (p < .01), with no differences detected within either cluster (p > .05), and a similar pattern within the oldest cohort. Assessment tasks and the cohorts represented changed across courses, however, leaving the extent of improvement in research competence unresolved. The grades describe performance on successive course requirements. The joint display connects cohort-level perceptions with course-stage performance; it does not test appreciation as a cause of individual grade improvement.

Delayed appreciation also sharpens the theoretical argument. The authors contrast this pattern with their reading of Kirkpatrick and learning-transfer models, which they characterize as treating appreciation as an immediate response to a learning experience [43,53]. Students here entered medical education after secondary school and encountered unfamiliar research content across an extended module. Their later recognition of its relevance suggests that curriculum evaluation should attend to when learners have opportunities to use knowledge. The 8A-Model directs attention to that timing while allowing application and appreciation to overlap with continuing acquisition and assimilation.

Undergraduate medical programs should provide opportunities to conduct supervised research as an integral part of research education [59,60]. Authentic projects give students a purpose for applying methods and a context for learning research practices with expert guidance. Teaching should connect earlier content to project decisions and support reflection on experience and existing mental models [63]. These actions preserve the module's emphasis on guided participation and purposeful revisiting of content. Implementation requires continuity between methods courses and project work, together with mentors able to support students' questions and developing judgments.

The study's limitations concern how this developmental interpretation was constructed and where it applies. Three cohort focus groups provided different perspectives on the curriculum, rather than repeated interviews with the same students; narratives were not linked to individual grade changes. Member checking involved four participants from the oldest cohort, rather than independent prospective validation. The single institution and nonexperimental design leave the contribution of project work relative to other influences unresolved. Longitudinal studies across programs should examine how application, appreciation and assessed competence develop together, and investigate subsequent research participation, careers and clinical practice. Patient outcomes require separate investigation.

## Conclusion

The 8A-Model interprets research learning in this undergraduate medical curriculum as an iterative process in which application brings the value of earlier instruction into view. Students' accounts and course-stage grade patterns support that curriculum-level interpretation. Comparable programs should integrate supervised projects with methods instruction and guided reflection. The model's educational contribution is to connect the acquisition of research knowledge with purposeful participation in research.
