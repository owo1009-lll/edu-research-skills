# Rationale and evidence record

## Task and one-screen argument, prepared before the first draft

This is a new, English secondary writing exercise: Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion only, 600?750 words excluding references. The source is the supplied factual packet for Kestin et al. (2025), not a new study conducted by this writer. No target journal, new empirical analysis, web search or manuscript-wide submission preparation is requested. Markdown is the requested delivery format.

**Educational problem ? evidence ? judgement ? scope.** Teachers deciding how students first encounter difficult course content need evidence about specific instructional arrangements. The relevant distinction is a deliberately structured tutor versus open-ended chatbot access. In the published crossover experiment, students receiving the tutor package scored higher on immediate topic quizzes than students receiving an experienced active-learning class using the same content. The contribution is evidence that this designed arrangement can improve initial topic learning even against a substantive classroom comparator. Its ingredients were bundled; feedback and flexible pacing remain possible explanations. The teaching implication is to evaluate supported initial learning as one role for such tutoring, while testing any proposed combination with collaborative classroom work. Delayed retention, transfer, wider settings and implementation costs remain open.
