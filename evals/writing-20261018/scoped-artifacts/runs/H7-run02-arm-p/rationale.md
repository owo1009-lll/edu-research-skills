## Author's argument, reconstructed before drafting

The educational problem is how undergraduate medical students come to understand and use research, and what role curriculum-based project work should play. The original argument moves from the 8A-Model's proposed learning milestones to an early/later course-grade contrast; interprets application as the turning point in appreciation and engagement; uses situated learning and a community of practice to justify authentic, supervised research; compares delayed appreciation with the authors' reading of evaluation/transfer models; and recommends experiential research modules. It ends by projecting longer-term professional and patient benefits and calling for longitudinal and multisite work.

The defensible contribution is an evidence-informed framework connecting students' accounts of initially unclear relevance with later accounts of application, appreciation and articulation in a sustained undergraduate research curriculum. The grade pattern offers convergent curriculum-level evidence, not an individually linked mechanism. Preserve the authors' strong practical judgment that programs should provide supervised research opportunities, their conceptual vocabulary and their iterative understanding of learning. Repair claims of required sequential milestones, appreciation-driven grade improvement, gender equivalence and observed downstream healthcare benefits.

## Evidence supporting the central judgment

- B003–B041: a compulsory five-semester module progresses from epidemiology, biostatistics and methods to supervised projects; situated learning and participation in a community of practice supply the educational rationale.
- B042–B108: participant 6.1 describes early confusion/disconnection; 7.2 and 8.2 describe relevance during projects; 9.2 describes professional communication with supervisors; 3.1 and 7.2 describe appraisal; 14.3 and 12.3 describe accomplishment and perceived transfer.
- Figure 2: research-course means are 3.01, 2.94, 2.95, 3.35 and 3.58 on the 0–4 GPA scale. Reported early/later comparisons and a similar oldest-cohort pattern support a course-stage contrast.
- Figures 1 and 3: Attend–Acquire, Accumulate–Assimilate, Apply–Appreciate and Articulate–Affect organize the authors' interpretation; the text allows iteration and overlap.

## Evidence restricting the judgment

- Course assessments differ, cohort composition changes, and not all students completed all courses. Grades are not an invariant measure of research competence.
- Three focus groups, one per cohort, reconstruct experience; they are not repeated within-person interviews. The joint display does not match each interviewee's account to their own grade change.
- There is no comparison program or randomized project-work intervention. Convergence cannot distinguish appreciation as a cause from other explanations of later grades.
- All-course GPA incorporates the research courses. Its fluctuations do not supply an independent comparator demonstrating curriculum integration.
- Member checking by four oldest-cohort participants is feedback on the framework, not prospective validation.
- Patient outcomes, healthcare costs and later research careers were not measured. Transfer/proficiency claims are participants' accounts.
- The single-institution, secondary-school-entry MBBS context defines the setting in which the framework was developed.

## Author/source queries retained outside the prose

1. Course availability is inconsistent: Methods describes five/three/one courses for oldest/middle/newest cohorts, whereas Results/Table 2 has N=158 for semesters 1–2, N=92 for 3–4 and N=54 for 5. No reconciliation has been invented. Avoid denominator-specific claims in the shortened text.
2. Gender claims lack supplied estimates and adequate inferential detail. Request the relevant analyses before reinstating the claim. The admission-standardization mechanism is speculative and is omitted.
3. Obtain and read the cited Kirkpatrick and learning-transfer originals before presenting an independent theoretical comparison. The revision attributes the comparison to the source authors.
4. Grade intervals are depicted without numerical endpoints; no endpoints are inferred from the figure and interval overlap is not treated as a test.
5. Original numbered references are not an independently read reference library. The shortened exercise cites its supplied source; omitted numbers are not declared erroneous or verified. Detailed external-literature claims are not expanded.
