# Writing rationale

## Main line established before drafting

The educational decision is whether to replace rereading worked examples with a six-week low-stakes retrieval routine when delayed conceptual transfer is the priority. The class-randomised comparison addresses this question in lower-secondary science, with equal content, scheduled lesson time and subsequent teacher explanation. The primary delayed estimate remains compatible with little difference and an educationally relevant benefit; the secondary immediate result favours retrieval. The contribution is a better-grounded classroom decision that distinguishes the learning target, the opportunity to reconstruct an explanation, pupils' experience of ease, and preparation demands. Immediate benefit warrants investigating its durability and permits conditional use for a short-term purpose; it does not justify presenting delayed benefit as established.

## Evidence supporting and qualifying the position

Supporting evidence: allocation of classes within schools; class as randomisation unit in analysis; adjustment for school and baseline performance; matched content, lesson time and subsequent teacher explanation; planned procedures; positive immediate estimate; recorded attempts in 81% of scheduled retrieval activities. Qualifying evidence: primary delayed interval crosses zero and includes worthwhile benefit; no equivalence margin; no test of the difference between immediate and delayed effects; attempt completion says nothing about quality; ease is a self-report with no validated learning measure based on it; preparation medians are descriptive; 24 missing pupils in total, with balance insufficient to establish ignorable missingness; three-school setting; comparison studies differ in outcomes, timing, populations and allocation.
