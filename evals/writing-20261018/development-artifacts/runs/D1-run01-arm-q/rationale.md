# Writing rationale

## Main line recorded before drafting

The educational decision is whether to replace rereading worked examples with a six-week retrieval routine when delayed conceptual transfer is the priority. The planned class-randomised comparison supplies an imprecise primary estimate at three weeks and a positive secondary immediate estimate. Our central judgment is that the evidence supports a purpose-specific use of retrieval while leaving a general replacement decision unresolved. The conceptual contribution is to separate provision of an opportunity to reconstruct, perceived ease, immediate performance and durable transfer when judging a classroom routine. The manuscript will preserve the authors' priority for delayed transfer without dismissing the immediate finding.

## Evidence that supports the argument

- The study planned its procedures before examining outcomes, randomly allocated classes within schools, matched content and scheduled time, supplied the same subsequent teacher explanation, and analysed the class as the randomisation unit with school and baseline adjustment.
- Immediate conceptual transfer favoured retrieval: adjusted d = 0.24, 95% CI [0.09, 0.39]. This supports the stated short-term teaching purpose.
- The classroom process record documents attempts before example checking in 81% of scheduled retrieval activities; it identifies an opportunity for reconstruction.
- R1 measured delayed factual recall in a university laboratory; R2 measured immediate conceptual performance in schools. Neither card directly settles the question of delayed classroom conceptual transfer.

## Evidence and boundaries that constrain the argument

- Primary delayed transfer: adjusted d = 0.08, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.23]. The interval includes little difference and a benefit relevant to the teaching question; no equivalence margin was specified.
- No test compared the immediate and delayed effects. The two estimates cannot establish decline, disappearance or retention of the immediate advantage.
- Attempt completion provides no information about correctness or depth. Ease was not a validated learning measure; no motivation scale, interviews or mediation analysis were supplied.
- Preparation medians of 10 and 6 minutes per lesson are descriptive and do not establish cost-effectiveness.
- Of 480 enrolled pupils, 456 completed both tests, with 12 missing in each condition. Equal counts do not establish ignorable missingness. The evidence concerns three schools and the supplied intervention and follow-up periods.
- R1 supplies no causal age-moderation evidence; R2 has teacher-selected conditions, no delayed test and an unmeasured proposed mechanism.

## Terminology

| Preferred term | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| retrieval routine / retrieval | Attempt an explanation before checking the worked example |
| rereading | Study the same worked example throughout the activity |
| conceptual transfer | Target learning outcome, qualified by immediate or delayed timing |
| delayed conceptual transfer | Primary outcome measured three weeks after intervention |
| immediate conceptual transfer | Secondary outcome measured immediately |
| reconstruction | Conceptual description of producing an explanation; productive reconstruction was not measured |
| attempt completion | Recorded participation in an attempt, not accuracy or depth |
| ease | Self-reported experience during activity, not a validated learning outcome |
| preparation time | Descriptive teacher-recorded median minutes per lesson |
| d | Supplied standardised adjusted difference; no additional effect-size calculation |
| CI | Confidence interval; define once in prose |
