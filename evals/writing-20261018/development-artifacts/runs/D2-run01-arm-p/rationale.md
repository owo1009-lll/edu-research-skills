## Author's argument before revision

The educational question is whether receiving feedback also affords a student a usable opportunity to ask why a judgement matters. The introduction distinguishes delivery from answerability and uses [C1] to show the limited question answered by response speed. The discussion moves from opening and revision records to interviews that make the distinction concrete, including a failed opportunity to attend. Its educational rationale is reciprocal accountability: students expected to justify their academic decisions need access to reasons for consequential judgements. The conclusion accordingly says institutions **should protect a route for questioning feedback in assessed work**. This is a normative position, not an estimated learning effect.

Preserve that progression, the term *answerability*, the headings, the distinction between delivery and reasons, and the warranted **should**. The author's human-only generalisation and causal interpretation of descriptive records are separable errors, not reasons to replace the argument with technology evaluation.

## Evidence and counter-evidence

Supporting material: both channels included the same pre-existing teacher question session; two different students, one in each channel, obtained reconsideration of a comment; twelve interviewees reported a question they could discuss. The normative premise is supplied by the author: responsibility for academic decisions requires access to reasons for consequential feedback. Together these support a conceptual distinction and a reason to protect accessible questioning, not channel superiority.

Qualifying material: four interviewees reported timetable clashes; sessions being offered therefore does not establish their accessibility to each student. Channel choice was voluntary at one institution, interviews involved sixteen volunteers, no adjusted group or causal analysis was performed, and neither attainment nor confidence was measured. Opening logs establish opening rather than attentive reading; revisions cannot be linked to particular comments. [C1] records faster first responses at another institution, without evidence about questioning or learning. These boundaries must remain visible where they affect the claim.
