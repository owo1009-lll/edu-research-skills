## Author argument reconstructed from the original draft

A quick response does not necessarily let a student question its relevance. Answerability names an arrangement in which the student can ask for reasons and receive an accountable response, including correction when appropriate. The author links that arrangement to the educational expectation that students justify their own decisions. Both feedback channels retained a teacher question session. The contrasting interview accounts illustrate how a comment can be questioned, including a teacher's own judgement, while timetable clashes show why nominal availability is insufficient. The supported conclusion is that institutions should protect an accessible route for questioning consequential feedback in assessed work. This is a normative commitment supported by a stated educational premise; it does not depend on a demonstrated learning effect.

## Evidence supporting and bounding the claim

Supporting evidence and premises: the author supplies the educational premise of reciprocal accountability; two distinct interview cases show corrections to an automated recommendation and a teacher's recommendation; 12 interviewees describe a question they could discuss; the teacher question session was shared by both channels and already existed.

Countervailing evidence and boundaries: four interviewees wanted to ask but reported clashes, so offering a session is not sufficient evidence of access for everyone; channel self-selection prevents treating groups as comparable; clicks record opening rather than attentive reading; coded revisions are not evidence that any specific comment caused the change; there was no adjusted comparison, causal analysis, attainment test or confidence measure; voluntary interviews do not estimate population prevalence; C1 addresses only first-response delivery. These distinctions are incorporated where their interpretation matters, rather than appended as repeated general disclaimers.
