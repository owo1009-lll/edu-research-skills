## Scope and author argument (recorded before the first draft)

Task: revise and compress the supplied English author draft to 450-550 manuscript words, preserving the three headings, the central concept and the author's supported position. This is a teaching simulation using a fictional record. No target journal, external research, new citations, new analysis or Word output is requested.

The author's argument runs from the inadequacy of response speed as an account of feedback, through the distinction between delivery, revision and questioning, to a responsibility institutions should protect in assessed work. Answerability means that students can ask why a comment matters and receive an accountable explanation, including correction where appropriate. It belongs to the feedback arrangement. The normative premise is that students who must justify academic decisions need access to reasons for consequential judgements. The strength of this premise is independent of whether the study establishes a learning effect.

The introduction defines the distinction and uses [C1] only to delimit what response-speed evidence answers. The discussion uses opening logs, coded revisions and voluntary interview accounts for their different purposes, including two distinct students' cases and the timetable obstacle. The conclusion retains the institutional obligation. The strongest contribution within the supplied material is a practical conceptual distinction: a delivered comment or a revised submission does not itself establish an opportunity to question the judgement. No new theoretical framework or general technology-evaluation agenda is needed.

## Evidence that supports and qualifies the argument

Supports: both channels retained the same teacher question session; twelve interviewees described a discussable question; the terminology and paragraph-order cases show recommendations reconsidered; the author's stated educational premise supplies the reason for an institutional obligation. The shared questioning arrangement can be discussed across comment sources.

Qualifies: four interviewees reported timetable clashes; attendance opportunities were uneven even though sessions existed. Sixteen voluntary interviews provide accounts, not population rates. The students selected channels at one institution. Opening logs measure opening, and the revision counts do not link comments to revisions. No adjusted comparison, causal analysis, attainment test or confidence measure was collected. The shared session predates the study, so protecting and improving access extends an existing arrangement rather than introducing a tested new intervention.
