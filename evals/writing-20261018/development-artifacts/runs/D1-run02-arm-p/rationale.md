# Writing rationale

## Scope and main argument (recorded before drafting)

New English writing: Introduction, Discussion and Conclusion only; 800–1,000 words, Markdown only. The question is approved, so no further confirmation is needed. The source is the fictional D1 study record and its two fictional comparison cards. No network, empirical reanalysis, new references, measurements, or invented study procedures are authorised. The supplied skill and its relevant relative references guide writing, not the manuscript's evidence base.

**Question → evidence → judgement → scope:** Should a six-week retrieval routine replace rereading worked examples when the intended outcome is delayed conceptual transfer? The class-randomised comparison estimates a small positive delayed difference with an interval spanning little difference and a benefit relevant to this decision. It supports an immediate advantage on a secondary outcome, but the difference between time-specific effects was not tested. Retain delayed transfer as the replacement criterion; distinguish a conditional short-term instructional use from a claim of durable superiority. The contribution is a classroom comparison that makes the decision depend on the intended learning outcome while distinguishing a reconstruction opportunity from productive reconstruction. Scope: lower-secondary science, three schools, the tested routine and timing; uncertainty from precision and missing outcomes remains.

## Evidence supporting the argument

- Allocation was random by class within school; the analysis used that unit and accounted for school and baseline performance.
- Content, scheduled lesson time and the subsequent teacher explanation were common to both conditions; the routine varied whether pupils attempted an explanation before checking the worked example.
- The delayed primary result is d = 0.08, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.23]; the immediate secondary result is d = 0.24, 95% CI [0.09, 0.39].
- [R1] addresses delayed factual recall in a university laboratory; [R2] addresses immediate conceptual performance in schools. Their distinct outcomes and designs motivate the specific unanswered question, not a claim of a field-wide gap.
- Attempt completion in 81% of scheduled retrieval activities supplies implementation evidence about an opportunity to reconstruct. Ease and teacher preparation records inform distinct practical considerations.

## Evidence constraining the argument

- The primary interval includes little difference and educationally relevant benefit; no equivalence margin was prespecified.
- Immediate and delayed effects were not compared directly. A smaller point estimate at the later test does not establish decline or disappearance of an effect.
- Attempts were not scored for correctness or depth. No interview, motivation scale or mediation analysis was collected; effortful reconstruction remains a candidate explanation.
- Greater reported ease under rereading is not a validated learning measure.
- Preparation medians (10 versus 6 minutes per lesson) are descriptive, without an inferential comparison or a basis for a cost-effectiveness ranking.
- Of 480 enrolled pupils, 456 completed both tests; 12 were missing in each condition. Equal counts do not establish ignorable missingness.
- [R1] does not identify age as a causal moderator; [R2] involved teacher choice and had no delayed test.

## Terms

| Preferred term | Meaning and usage |
|---|---|
| retrieval routine | Attempt an explanation without the worked example, then check that example; use “retrieval” as an ordinary shortened form |
| rereading | Study the same worked example throughout the activity |
| delayed conceptual transfer | Primary outcome, tested three weeks after the intervention |
| immediate conceptual transfer | Secondary outcome, tested immediately |
| reconstruction | Conceptual account of what the attempted explanation invites; productive reconstruction was not measured |
| attempt completion | Completion of an explanation attempt; neither correctness nor depth |
| reported ease | Experience during the activity, not a learning or motivation scale |
| preparation time | Teacher-recorded median minutes per lesson; descriptive |
| d | Standardised adjusted difference between the study conditions |
| CI | Confidence interval; define on first use |

## Cross-section consistency plan

The Introduction asks about delayed transfer without anticipating a confirmatory answer. The Discussion first reports the unresolved primary result and then interprets the secondary immediate benefit. The Conclusion repeats the unresolved primary judgement and the conditional short-term use. Limits of equivalence, change over time and mechanism interpretation stay beside those claims; missingness and school scope are concentrated in the limitations paragraph. No abstract, methods, results, full bibliography or Word file is requested. Unnumbered section headings allow integration with later methods/results sections without assigning their numbering.

## Revision and final audit

One complete revision followed the saved first draft and focused AI review. It made the educational decision more direct, separated the R1 comparison from the immediate-result paragraph, clarified the study's contribution, and converted the implication into an affirmative short-term recommendation. The preparation paragraph now treats the absence of an inferential test and the insufficiency of preparation time alone for cost-effectiveness as distinct issues. An explicit limitations unit groups missingness and school scope. These edits address clarity and inference, not new evidence or a change to the authors' main question.

The final manuscript was checked against every row of the evidence and terminology tables. The primary outcome remains delayed conceptual transfer; the immediate finding remains secondary.
