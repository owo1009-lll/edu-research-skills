## Revisions to make

1. **Correct language about mediation.** “No mediation analysis was collected” confuses an analysis with a collected measure. Replace it with “the study did not test mediation.” This preserves the absence of mechanism evidence without inventing a method or a result.
2. **Use a stable primary-outcome name.** Replace “delayed conceptual-transfer benefit” with “benefit in delayed conceptual transfer.” The hyphenation is grammatical, but consistent naming makes the outcome easier to track.
3. **Improve the comparative-design sentence.** “Strengthens this comparison relative to teacher choice” is vague about which comparison is meant. Specify that class randomization adds evidence less vulnerable to teacher selection of the routine; leave incomplete follow-up addressed in the limitations paragraph.
4. **Clarify the substantive recommendation.** Make the connection between the immediate result and retention of the routine more explicit: teachers can use it to pursue an immediate conceptual objective where preparation fits, while durable transfer remains the criterion for replacing an existing routine for that purpose. This is the supplied author position, not a recommendation inferred solely from a nonsignificant delayed result.
5. **Repair the conclusion's wording.** “A less effortless activity” is awkward and risks treating reported ease as an objective effort measure. End with the distinction between opportunity to reconstruct and demonstrated learning; avoid turning the ease item into an established effort mechanism.
6. **Smooth a few sentence transitions.** Combine closely related clauses when this improves the argument, not to target corpus sentence-length medians. Keep necessary statistical detail because these sections must be interpretable without a separately supplied results section.
