# Writing rationale

## Main line established before drafting

**Question:** Does the six-week low-stakes retrieval routine improve delayed conceptual transfer relative to rereading worked examples in lower-secondary science?

**What the evidence answers:** The primary delayed estimate is small and imprecise: adjusted d = 0.08, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.23]. It accommodates little difference and a benefit relevant to the teaching question. The secondary immediate estimate favours retrieval: d = 0.24, 95% CI [0.09, 0.39]. The difference between time points was not tested. Random allocation occurred at class level within schools; the analysis respected this unit and adjusted for school and baseline performance.

**Core judgement:** Replacing an existing routine to improve durable conceptual transfer requires evidence on that outcome. This study leaves that benefit unresolved while supporting a short-term instructional use of retrieval under appropriate lesson and preparation conditions. Its educational contribution is a concrete separation of an opportunity to reconstruct, perceived ease, immediate performance, and delayed transfer in evaluating a classroom routine. It does not establish a new mechanism or a boundary condition.

**Scope:** This is closed-evidence fictional teaching material. The manuscript covers only introduction, discussion and conclusion in English. No hypothesis, analysis, measure, causal moderator, external citation, journal claim, or real bibliography will be added. No network, subagents, or other runs will be used. Markdown only. The user has approved proceeding directly.

## Evidence that supports the judgement

- The intended outcome and comparison were supplied; procedures were planned before outcomes were examined.
- Random allocation within each of three schools, the same content, equal scheduled lesson time, and the same subsequent teacher explanation strengthen the comparison between routines.
- The delayed interval is necessary for a decision about replacement; the immediate interval supports a short-term benefit.
- The attempt record establishes that the opportunity to reconstruct was often taken, not the quality of reconstruction.
- The preparation record supplies a practical resource consideration without a cost-effectiveness estimate.
- [R1] and [R2] each address a related question but neither combines this study's classroom comparison and delayed conceptual-transfer outcome.

## Evidence restricting stronger conclusions

- The delayed interval includes zero, negative values, and a relevant positive benefit; no equivalence margin was prespecified.
- Different estimates and different significance patterns across time do not establish a decline in effect.
- Attempts were recorded in 81% of scheduled retrieval activities; correctness and depth were not measured.
- Ease is a reported activity experience, not a validated measure of learning or motivation.
- No interview, motivation scale or mediation analysis was collected.
- Preparation medians are descriptive and have no inferential test.
- 456 of 480 pupils completed both post-tests. Equal missing counts (12 per condition) do not establish ignorable missingness.
- Three schools and a three-week delayed follow-up limit scope beyond the sampled setting and time horizon.
- [R1] measured factual recall in university laboratory participants; [R2] used teacher choice and had no delayed test. Neither identifies age moderation or a reconstruction mediator.
